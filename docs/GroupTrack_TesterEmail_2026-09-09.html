What is in it, for anyone you send it to

The route planner. Tell it where you are starting, how far you want to ride and how fast — it searches your trail network and comes back with several complete options side by side. Each one described: distance, riding time, and every summit and landmark you will pass with the mile you will reach it.

It finds trails nobody has mapped. Import your recorded tracks and GroupTrack identifies the ground no published survey covers, then adds it as trail the planner can route on. My own 88 tracks produced over 1,800 new trails — about 2,400 miles that no public source has. Those rides can only be found here, because no other app knows the trail is there.

Over 145,000 trails in Utah alone, and trail data is available for all fifty states.

Everything works offline, and if you pair a Meshtastic radio your whole group shows on one map with no cell service.

Two honest notes

Utah is where the data is most complete. It is where I ride and where this has been tested hardest. Other states have trail data, but the public and private land information is still Utah only, so the ownership filtering will not work the same elsewhere. That is next on the list.

Import your tracks first. The planner works from the trails it knows, and your own rides add ground no map has. Do that before you judge the route planner — it is the difference between seeing what this does and seeing a fraction of it.

Keep the feedback coming. What you like, what you would want added or changed. It is the reason this is any good.

— Fred

Two claims I changed, and why

  • "All 50 states available for free downloads" → "trail data is available for all fifty states", with the ownership caveat. ⚠ Land ownership exists for Utah only, so a rider in Colorado gets trails with no public/private classification. Overselling that to the people most likely to try another state is how thirty engaged testers become thirty disappointed ones.
  • ⚠ "140,000 trails" → "over 145,000". The real figure is 147,836 including 1,872 rider trails. Rounding down is safer and still large.
  • ⛔ No price language anywhere. Fine in an email, but keep it out if any of this text is reused on the store listing — it is a metadata policy violation there.
