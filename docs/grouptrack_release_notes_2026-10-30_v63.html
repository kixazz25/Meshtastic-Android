GroupTrack 2.7c — internal testing

27 October 2026 (build c) · 26 October 2026 (build b) · One Map: one map, every tool, a cleaner screen · with internal testers before release

2.7c replaces the ride map and the planning map with one map. Everything you could do on either map is on it, reached from a few buttons around the edge.

Navigation is organised by what you are doing. Each button opens one job, and the steps of a job sit together behind it — every map task (download, remove, recover maps, import trails and features) under Map, everything about rides under Rides, building a route under Add Route, the radio under RADIO SETUP, what shows on the map under FILTER and how it is drawn under MAP KEY. In earlier versions the same jobs were spread across two maps and several menus. One map with a menu and buttons laid out like the outdoor apps riders already know — Gaia GPS and onX — makes GroupTrack far easier to find your way around: you go to the button for the job, and everything for that job is there.

Two modes, one button. Mode on the map switches between them (not while you are recording):

  • Android only — one rider, no radio. Your tablet's GPS places your cart; REC records your ride, and the readout shows MY CART. Everything else — maps, trails, routes, rides, search — works the same.
  • Group — several riders, each with a mesh radio. CHK IN checks you in to the ride with your role, then records; the map shows every cart in the group, GRP Awareness shows your radio link, and View: Group / View: My Cart (in Add Route's place once you are checked in) switches the readout between the whole group and your own cart.

Why the maps are changing. The satellite and topo maps came from providers — Google and Esri among them — whose terms do not allow an app to download their map pictures and keep them on your tablet for use offline. GroupTrack depends on exactly that, so it has to stop. The new map is built from OpenStreetMap open data, which is licensed to be copied and kept as long as its sources are credited, and the ground shape comes from public elevation data (USGS and others). The credits now show at the bottom of the map. This is also why the conversion removes the old map pictures from your tablet rather than keeping them alongside.

What you gain. Downloads about ten times faster. Maps that take a tenth of the space or less — our test tablet went from 18.4 GB to under 0.8 GB for the same areas. 3D, with the elevation of the ground — hills, canyons and shaded slopes. A map that tilts, so you look out ahead across the ground the way you see it from the seat. And a map that turns with you, so the way you are riding is always up.

GroupTrack moves to one map: a vector map (OpenFreeMap, from OpenStreetMap) with the shape of the ground underneath it. It replaces the satellite and the two topo maps. The map is drawn on your tablet instead of shown as pictures, so it is sharp at every zoom, it can tilt into 3D and turn with you, and your maps take a small fraction of the space and download time. Everything you have made — trails, tracks, routes, waypoints and rides — stays exactly as it is. Items marked being tested are not finished.

NEW One map instead of three

The map type button (SAT, TOPO, TOPO+) is gone: there is one map, with roads, trails, water, places and their names, and it works with no signal. For the same areas, our test tablet's maps went from 18.4 GB of pictures to 392 MB of map plus 372 MB of ground shape. The map 30 Oct

What it means for you: more of the country fits on your tablet, downloads finish in minutes, and the map stays sharp however close you zoom.

NEW Your maps are converted when you update

The first time the new version starts, GroupTrack writes down what your maps cover, queues the new maps for exactly those areas, and then removes the old map pictures to free the space. Downloads run in MAP DOWNLOADS as usual; if the tablet is turned off or GroupTrack is closed, the queue carries on where it stopped. If anything fails, Map › Restart map conversion downloads whatever is still missing — as often as you need, without downloading twice what you already have. A tablet with no maps simply starts as a new install. Updating to one map coming in 2.7e

What it means for you: you do not redraw a single area — your maps come back on their own, in the new form.

IMPORTANT Radios report every 30 m — re-apply your radio setup

GroupTrack set radios to send a moving cart's position only every 100 m (328 ft). At trail speed that is one report every 15–22 seconds, and the lead cart's track was drawn as long straight lines that cut across the trail. Your radio keeps the old setting until it is set up again: check in to a ride, or RADIO SETUP › Configure radio. Import Trails — by State or by Area (New)

NEW Import Trails now asks how you want to choose your ground. One button on the Map Features panel, two ways in:

  • By State — pick any state from the list. Every available source for that state is processed. Use this when you're planning a trip somewhere new.
  • By Area — draw a box on the map. GroupTrack works out which states your box reaches and processes every source that covers it. Your box can cross state lines — a ride that runs from Utah into Arizona brings in both.

Either way there is no source checklist. Every source that covers your ground runs, because that is what lets one source fill in what another is missing.

Import Progress Detail

NEW Every import now records what happened to each record, not just how many were added. Per source: how many were read, how many fell inside your state or area, how many you already had, how many were added, and how many could not be processed.

The summary still shows one number — records added. The detail sits behind the source name if you want it, and it is written into the import record on your device either way, so it can be looked up later.

CHANGED Each import run now carries an identifier, so a run can be identified exactly when something needs looking into.
