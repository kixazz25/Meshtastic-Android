V2.7 SCOPING — vector rendering & the MapLibre question
2026-07-26 · INTERNAL · kickoff input for the CONVOY TRACKING REWRITE · nothing built, nothing decided
Why this document exists. The question came up mid-release on 07-26 while 2.6d was uploading, and it turned out to bear directly on 2.7. Fred's framing is the reason it is scoped here rather than deferred: "2.7 is a rewrite of convoy tracking functionality — may be the point we need to introduce these discussions." That is correct, and it changes the economics completely (see ⭐ THE SEQUENCING ARGUMENT). This is analysis, not a plan. Nothing here is committed.

⭐⭐ THE MEASUREMENT THAT STARTED IT

The 07-26 delete-area log showed a single DEL SAT job walking three separate stores:

storez18 removedwhat it holds
SAT10,295base imagery — photographic
SAT_LABELS_TRANSPORT10,292road names
SAT_LABELS_PLACES10,294place names

Near-perfect thirds. Fred's read, and it is the whole insight: "it is 65,000 tiles because of the three layers. if vectors eliminated the street name and points of interest overlays we are downloading 1/3 of what we download as sat images."

⚠ TWO OF THE THREE ARE TEXT, NOT PHOTOGRAPHY. Road names and place labels are the single most compressible thing on a map — a few hundred strings and coordinates per tile — and they are currently delivered as photographs of words, at the same cost per tile as satellite imagery, re-fetched at every zoom level.

The arithmetic, on bar 10

sourcetodaywith vector labels + vector topo
SAT base imagery~21,700~21,700 raster (unavoidable)
SAT transport labels~21,700vector
SAT place labels~21,700vector
TOPO21,000vector
TOPO+21,000vector
RASTER TOTAL107,000~21,700

≈80% reduction — and it stacks on the corridor work already shipped. Bar 10 went from 1,000,000+ tiles (bbox) to 107,000 (corridor) this week. Vector labels would take the same track to ~21,700.

⚠ WHY VECTOR IS SMALL — the mechanism, not the format. Vector tiles stop at ~z14 and the client renders deeper by scaling geometry. Since tile counts QUADRUPLE per zoom, z15–18 is roughly 98% of everything downloaded. Vector does not shrink those levels; it removes them. That is why a whole-state vector extract is comparable in size to a handful of today's raster corridors.

⭐⭐⭐ THE SEQUENCING ARGUMENT — why 2.7 and not later

The dominant cost of this work is NOT MapLibre. It is operating on frozen code.

ConvoyScreen.kt is frozen to protect the Kotlin↔JS↔Leaflet↔self-saving-setView↔shared-MapStateStore tangle. A convoy tracking rewrite has to open exactly that tangle anyway.

So the choice is not "rewrite convoy now, renderer later." It is: rewrite that layer ONCE with MapLibre as the target, or TWICE.

  • Doing it once is dramatically cheaper.
  • The tracking rewrite then gets built against rotation, pitch and data-driven styling rather than being ported onto them afterwards.
  • Convoy tracking may benefit more than the map does: live cart positions are the textbook case for data-driven styling — heading, speed, staleness and lead-cart status driving symbol rotation and colour from feature properties, instead of tearing down and rebuilding markers. A tilted terrain view is also arguably more useful while riding than while planning.

⚠ This is the single strongest argument in this document. Everything else is secondary to it.

⭐⭐⭐ THE CROSS-PLATFORM MULTIPLIER (the point that was nearly missed)

Per [[grouptrack-crossplatform]], the 07-04 planning session established that the convoy/planning presentation is ALREADY web (Leaflet/HTML/JS in a WebView), and that the leverage for iPad and PC lives in the web layer, not in Swift. SwiftUI was rejected for presentation precisely because it gives zero reuse of that layer.

⭐ MapLibre GL JS is a WEB library. It runs identically in an Android WebView, an iPad WKWebView, and a PC browser.

So a renderer swap done in the shared web layer is not an Android cost. It is a one-time change to the ~90% presentation layer that all three surfaces inherit:

  • Android — the immediate target.
  • iPad — inherits it free, whichever way the A/B mesh-source decision goes. The iPad app is a native shell hosting this same web layer.
  • PC planning app — inherits it free, and gains the most: terrain, pitch and vector styling in a plain browser, with no offline-tile constraint at all.

⚠ The corollary is a REAL RISK: doing the tracking rewrite on Leaflet bakes Leaflet into the layer iPad and PC are both waiting on. That is a decision with a long tail, made by omission.

Also aligns with the service-layer mandate: the renderer is presentation. Swapping it should touch logic-free panels, not the service layer — and the degree to which it does NOT is a useful read on how entangled the current code really is (the 07-04 "logic-entanglement inventory" question, answered from a different angle).

What was actually validated (browser prototype, 07-26)

vector_label_prototype.html — Esri World Imagery raster + OSM vector labels + Esri's own raster label layers as toggles, over identical imagery. No build, no device, no touching frozen code.

  • CONFIRMED Esri raster imagery and OSM vector labels composite correctly. Fred: "the image overlays look perfect."
  • CONFIRMED — AND THE SURPRISE OSM vector labels carry MORE data than Esri's downloaded roads and places layers. Fred: "much more data in the vector labels then the downloaded esri roads and places db." ⚠ This was the OPPOSITE of the predicted outcome — the expectation was thin OSM coverage in remote Utah and a coverage-for-size trade. Instead: more content AND less storage.
  • CONFIRMED NEGATIVE OSM TRAIL coverage is sparse. Fred: "i see very few trails render." ⭐ So OSM is a BASEMAP, not a trail source. grouptrack.gpkg and the national trail model remain the authority. This closes a question that was genuinely open at the start of the discussion.
  • CONFIRMED 3D terrain works and Leaflet cannot do it at all — not tilt, not rotation, not terrain. Elevation comes from a separate raster-DEM source (terrarium encoding), capped ~z13, so a whole-state DEM is small.

⭐ Terrain-RGB is worth more than the 3D view. Those tiles encode metres-above-sea-level in RGB rather than a pre-rendered grey hillshade, so the CLIENT holds raw elevation. That enables slope-angle shading computed on device — which for an OHV app is arguably more useful than satellite imagery, and costs a fraction of it.

⛔ CORRECTIONS TO MY OWN ANALYSIS (recorded so they are not repeated)

  • ⛔ I leaned on Esri per-tile cost. Fred: "we pay nothing for esri downloads and data." That pillar is GONE. Any future argument for this work must stand on storage, download time and capability — not economics.
  • MapLibre is FREE — BSD, no API key, no usage tier. (Fred asked whether it carried a cost; it does not. The prototype uses it with no key.) The cost of this work is developer time on frozen code, nothing else.
  • ⛔ My first sizing was wrong by half. I said ~39% because I assumed satellite stays whole. Fred corrected it by pointing at the three-store split — two of the three are labels. The real figure is ~80%. The delete log had shown this hours earlier and I did not connect it.
  • Vector cannot replace satellite imagery. Aerial photography is inherently raster. This is physics, not tooling — no vector source substitutes for it, ever.

The five work items

  1. THE RENDERER (the real work). Leaflet cannot draw vector tiles. MapLibre GL JS in the same WebView. spatial_artifacts.js is rewritten: L.polyline calls become GeoJSON sources + layer specs; per-feature click handlers become queryRenderedFeatures (MapLibre gives NO DOM per feature). The data path from SpatialDbManager is UNCHANGED — MapLibre takes GeoJSON natively, exactly as Leaflet does.
  2. TILE INTERCEPTION. Same shape, different hook: MapLibre's addProtocol replaces the WebView request intercept. Still serving bytes out of MBTiles.
  3. ⚠ GLYPHS — the requirement that is easy to miss. MapLibre renders text from font PBFs fetched by URL. Offline means BUNDLING them in assets (a few MB per fontstack). There is no equivalent in the raster world, so nothing in the pipeline handles it today. Miss it and labels silently do not render offline.
  4. MAP STATE. MapLibre's camera adds bearing and pitch to centre/zoom — MapStateStore and the self-saving setView need rework. This is the piece nearest the frozen tangle.
  5. GETTING LABEL TILES ONTO THE DEVICE. planetiler --only-layers=place,poi,transportation_name,water_name,mountain_peak over a Utah bbox produces a labels-only tileset. Then a product decision: bundle it, or a "download this region" flow — one file once, versus per-corridor forever.

⚠ ONLY THEN drop the two overlay layers from SAT's entry in getDownloadSources(). That is a one-line config change — but it must come LAST, or satellite ships with no labels at all.

What does NOT change (important — nothing is obsoleted)

Fred asked directly whether this obsoletes the artifact data. It leverages it. The base layer and the artifacts are separate concerns sharing a canvas.

  • Untouched: grouptrack.gpkg and the national trail model · both databases · all three engines (ingestion, AWS bbox distribution, viewport lazy-load) · SpatialDbManager WKT storage and geom_hash identity · ArtifactListPanel, search, CRUD · the rating/share work · corridor downloads (they derive from YOUR geometry, not the base) · the queue, priorities, and the new delete worker (all format-agnostic — MBTiles stores blobs by z/x/y and does not care what is in them).
  • Satellite does not have to go. MapLibre handles raster and vector sources in ONE map, so SAT stays a toggleable raster layer exactly as today.
  • Your artifacts stay GeoJSON overlays. Tiles are a static read-only artifact — a poor fit for tracks the user records and edits. Tiling is for DISTRIBUTED REFERENCE data (the national trail model), not user-authored data.

⭐ The gain for artifacts: DATA-DRIVEN STYLING. MapLibre paint properties are expressions over feature properties — so the 1–5 star rating being added to track_properties, plus tracktype or shared, can drive colour and width directly from the data, with no extra layers and no per-feature style callbacks. Leaflet cannot do this natively. Demonstrated working in the prototype.

⚠ One real design tension: OSM's vector base ALREADY CONTAINS trails (highway=track, highway=path). You would have OSM trails underneath and curated GeoPackage trails on top — duplicated and possibly offset by metres. Filter them from the base style, or style them very faintly. The second option is an opportunity: faint "unverified" OSM trails become a discovery surface — showing riders what exists on the ground that is not yet curated, and showing you where the national trail model has gaps.

Sequencing (if it proceeds)

  1. PLANNING MAP FIRST. ALWAYS. MapLibre with EXISTING raster sources and EXISTING GeoJSON overlays — no vector tiles, no terrain. This proves the renderer swap in isolation. ⚠ Otherwise you are debugging a rendering swap AND a tracking rewrite simultaneously, and neither failure is attributable — the same one-unknown-at-a-time rule that governs the Mac/KMP sequencing.
  2. Add the vector label source alongside, so the on-device comparison matches what the browser prototype showed.
  3. Then drop the Esri label layers from getDownloadSources().
  4. Convoy last, and carefully — as part of the tracking rewrite, not before it.

Open questions

  • Label coverage at z16–18. The browser comparison was at mid-zoom. Vector labels come from a z14 tile and should render crisp at any zoom — but Esri's raster labels are densest at high zoom and that is the fair test. Not yet done.
  • Does TOPO/TOPO+ go vector too, or only the labels? The ~80% figure assumes both. Labels alone is ~40% and a much smaller change.
  • Distribution model for label tiles: bundled in the APK (bigger install, zero friction) vs downloadable region packs (smaller install, a new flow to build). Ties to V3.0 ride-package distribution.
  • Contours. OSM has none — they come from DEM. USGS 1/3 arc-second is the US source; OpenMapTiles also publishes worldwide contour datasets. Only matters if TOPO goes vector.
  • Does this pull the KMP/service-layer work forward? The renderer swap is a natural moment to test how cleanly presentation separates from logic — which is the 07-04 entanglement inventory by another name.

Ruled out

  • ⛔ Vector satellite imagery — does not exist, cannot exist.
  • ⛔ OSM as a trail data source — coverage confirmed sparse on device. Basemap only.
  • ⛔ Tiling user-authored artifacts — tiles are static and read-only; tracks and routes are edited constantly. GeoJSON overlays stay.
  • ⛔ Doing this as a standalone V2.6 task — weeks of surgery on frozen code, while SAT-frozen-at-15, the ETA at ~50×, and the markComplete refreshMode collision are all still open. It earns its place ONLY as part of the 2.7 tracking rewrite.
