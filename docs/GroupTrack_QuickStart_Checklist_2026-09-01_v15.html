5b · Turn off what you do not need — optional

Tap Map Keys (on the convoy map it is between Map Features and Help; on the planning map it is the legend button). This panel does two jobs.

It tells you what the colours mean. Anything you can ride is a shade of blue — the brighter the blue, the more certain the app is that it is a real riding route. A faint dashed blue line means the app knows something is there and nothing more.

And it lets you hide what you are not interested in:

  • Set Land to Public and the residential street grid disappears. Near a town this is the difference between a readable map and a mess.
  • Uncheck any row to hide that type of trail. Tap a column heading to hide the whole group.
  • Everything starts switched on, so if you never open this panel nothing changes.

What you choose is remembered for next time.

Planning a ride, step by step (2026-08-29)

The planner now asks one thing at a time. An overview explains what you will get; a second screen sets your distance and pace and shows the ride duration as you adjust them. After that each step puts a short prompt at the bottom of the screen and leaves the map clear for you to work on.

  • Trailhead — tap the waypoint where you will park, or press and hold to create one.
  • Points to cover — tap the closest trail to anywhere you want the ride to reach. A pin the planner cannot route to is removed and it tells you why.
  • Finish — a loop back to your trailhead, or a different end point.
  • Review — what is about to be built, then Continue to Compare.

Planning stays in portrait throughout. Every step offers Start Over.
