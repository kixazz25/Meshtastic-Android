Long version

For your own page and the groups that tolerate a proper post.

I've been building something and it's ready for people to try.

I ride Utah, same as most of you. And I got tired of two things: not knowing where the sweep was when the group strung out, and route apps that don't know about half the ground we actually ride.

So I built GroupTrack.

The part I'm proudest of. Import your old tracks — onX, Gaia, wherever you've kept them — and it finds the ground no published map covers and turns it into trail it can route on. My own 88 tracks produced about 2,400 miles of trail that no public source has.

Then you tell it what kind of ride you want. Start here, 55 to 80 miles, 12 to 18 mph. It searches and comes back with several complete options, laid out side by side, each one described — distance, riding time, and every summit you'll pass with the mile you'll hit it.

The other part. Pair a Meshtastic radio and everyone in the group is on your map, no cell service. Lead knows the sweep is coming, sweep knows nobody's sat at a junction.

Everything works offline. Trail data and satellite imagery live on your Android.

The state download brings in everything, not just motorized. Hiking and biking trails are available and selectable in the map keys, so you can turn them on for whatever you're doing that day.

Things worth knowing before you install:

Setting up a state pulls the whole trail dataset. Around 30 minutes and a lot of data — do it on wifi at home, not in the parking lot at the trailhead.

Group tracking needs a Meshtastic radio. Everything else works without one.

The trail data is strongest in Utah. That's where I ride and that's where I've tested it.

And it knows a trail is there — it doesn't know how wide it is or what's on it. Check the satellite view before you commit.

It's in early access and free for riders. I'm looking for feedback — what you like, and what you'd want added or changed.

Import your rides first. The planner works off the trails it knows, and your own tracks add ground no published map has. Import everything you've got and it'll find rides it couldn't find otherwise.

When you use the route planner, I'd especially like to know whether the rides it comes back with are ones you'd actually take.

Short version

For groups that dislike long posts, or a second post later in the week.

Been building a trail app for the last while and it's ready for people to try.

Two things it does that I couldn't find elsewhere: it turns your old recorded tracks into trails it can plan routes on — mine produced 2,400 miles no public map has — and it keeps your whole group on one map over mesh radio with no cell service.

Offline maps, route planning that gives you options rather than a line to follow, and a description of every ride it finds. The state download includes hiking and biking trails too, available and selectable alongside the motorized ones.

Early access, free for riders. Import your old tracks first — the planner uses them and finds rides it otherwise couldn't. I'd like to hear what you like and what you'd want added or changed.

Heads up: first setup downloads a state's trail data, about 30 minutes. Do it on wifi.

How to post it

  • ⭐ Your own page first, then share to the groups. A share from a member reads differently from a post dropped straight in — and you already work this way.
  • Check each group's link rules. Several UTV groups ban links in posts but allow them in comments. Body first, link in the first comment — that often gets better reach anyway.
  • One image with the post. The routes-compared screen is the one that makes people ask what it is. It explains itself and needs no caption.
  • ⚠ Don't post to all eight groups the same day. Two or three, spaced out. The same post appearing everywhere at once reads as a campaign, which is what these groups punish.

Two things to watch for in replies

  • "Do I have to buy a radio?" — will come up immediately. Answer is no, everything but group tracking works without one. Worth having ready rather than composing on the spot.
  • "Does it cover [state]?" — the honest answer is that the data is strongest in Utah. ⚠ Better to say so than to have a rider in Colorado install it, find thin data and leave a one-star.

⛔ Keep this out of the Play listing

Price language — "free", "free for riders" — is a metadata policy violation on Play and can get the app rejected. It is fine on Facebook, which is yours to say what you like on. The store listing says nothing about price at all, and that is deliberate.
