1 · The day in one line

Source map migration went from a reload engine to one question with three answers, and the code shrank with it.

The morning built and proved the destructive half. The afternoon and evening was design — and most of it was spent removing things. What shipped is smaller than what was written.

⭐ THE UNLOCK, and it was Fred's: "remove the tracks — the things we could put back." Every earlier design tried to preserve both storage shapes at once, which forced detection logic, decision matrices and per-item pickers. Take the tracks out and the area bounds become honest by themselves, and tracks cost ~10 minutes each to restore.

3 · The design, settled

1  DELETE AND START OVER    protect NOTHING     clear · rebuild around tracks · draw new areas
2  MOVE EVERYTHING          protect the AREA    delete track tiles · refresh area · re-import tracks
   (automatic, background)                      all downloads submitted at once; every image slowly
                                                replaced from the new source
3  MOVE ON MY SCHEDULE      protect EVERYTHING  nothing touched; convert manually, ground by ground,
                                                with Replace tiles ticked

⭐ No map-type question. "How were these downloaded?" only existed to route an automated refresh — which is just as well, because nothing on disk records how a store was built.

⭐ Clear is now only ever chosen on purpose. No case is forced into it, which is what removed the "are you riding soon?" question entirely.

⭐ The governing constraint (Fred): these screens process ALL or delete ALL. No partial conversion. That single rule removed the detection logic, the matrices and the pickers.

9 · The bigger idea, recorded late

⭐⭐ Fred: "I do not care about route. I am trying to create a method where a PRE-RIDE ROUTE CAN BE DISTRIBUTED AND IMPORTED WITH MAPS, allowing a rider to download accurate maps with efficient space utilisation BEFORE TRAILS ARE CAPTURED."

The route is the distribution unit. A leader plots the ride, emails it out, and each rider imports it and downloads exactly the corridor that ride needs. Nobody has to have ridden the ground, and nobody has to box a region.

⭐ Why it is accurate: a SNAPPED route's geometry IS trail geometry — snap-2 pulls the trail's own vector points — padded half a mile either side. Corridor-quality coverage for ground never ridden.

⭐ Email survives it. The snapping happened at authoring time, so the coordinates already follow the trail. What does not travel is the per-vertex lineId — a local DB reference. So a shared route downloads maps and draws perfectly; re-snapping is re-established locally by adding or removing a point.

What it needs: route geom_hash (a column, one call at insert, a backfill — computeGeomHash(wkt) exists and insertRoute already receives the WKT) · artifact TYPE carried alongside the hash so the worker looks in the right table · a distribution format and import path, mirroring track import's corridor option.

⛔ Without the hash, ConvoyCorridorWorker resolves against TRACKS, finds nothing, and fails quietly — the batch counts no-geometry as skipped.
