Full description

4,000 character limit. Draft below is ~2,050, leaving room for a price line and a hardware note once those are settled.

An off-road trail app for side-by-side, UTV, ATV, dirt bike and Jeep riders.

It holds the trails and the maps on your device, so it keeps working where there is no signal — which is most of where you ride.

Tell it the ride you want. Get rides worth taking.

Not a route you draw. Not somebody else's GPX file.

Say where you are starting, how far you want to go and how fast you ride. GroupTrack searches your trail network and comes back with several complete options — 74 miles, 77 miles, 68 miles — laid out side by side so you can compare them and pick one.

Each ride arrives described: distance, riding time, and every summit, spring and landmark you will pass, in the order you will reach them. Mile 8, Mountain Spring Peak. Mile 25, Pink Knolls. Mile 30, Red Hill.

Your ride history becomes trail you can plan on

Every app lets you import your old tracks. They sit on the map as lines you rode once.

GroupTrack does something else with them. Where a ride of yours crosses ground no published survey covers, it becomes a trail — part of your network, searchable, and available to the route planner as a piece it can build with.

So a connector you found three years ago stops being a line in your history and starts turning up in rides you never took, joined to trails you have not ridden yet.

One rider's collection of 88 tracks produced over 1,800 new trails covering 2,400 miles that no public source had. Those rides can only be found here, because no other app knows the trail is there.

Import everything you have — onX, Gaia, anywhere. The more of your riding it knows, the better the rides it can find.

Your whole group on one map, with no cell service

Pair a Meshtastic radio and every rider appears on your map in real time. No towers, no signal, no network subscription.

The lead knows the sweep is still coming. The sweep knows nobody has been left at a junction. Group span, rider count and who is at the tail, on screen, all day.

Everything works offline

Download your state's trail data and satellite or topographic imagery before you leave. Plan routes, follow them, record your ride and track your group with the phone in airplane mode.

Built to be learned in an afternoon

A Quick Start opens the first time you run it: 32 short exercises, each one a tap away from the manual section that explains it. Work through them at the kitchen table and you will know the app before you need it on the trail.

Honest about what it knows

GroupTrack knows a trail is there. It does not know how wide it is, what the surface is like, or what is on it. Check the satellite view before you commit, and check access and ownership the way you would anywhere else.

First run

Setting up a state downloads its full trail dataset — around 30 minutes and a lot of data. Do it on wifi, before the trip, not in the trailhead parking lot.

When the app loads your location, you are ready to play.

⛔ The group shot has to be retaken

The one in the collection is suburban New Hampshire — an insurance office, a banquet centre, Griffin Park — and it reads as a road map, not an OHV app. It also shows 0.0 mi span, 1 cart, which is a demo rather than a convoy.

Retaking it in New Hampshire is fine, with three conditions:

  • A dirt road or forest road, not a residential street. Nobody will identify the state; they will notice a driveway.
  • Real span on the HUD. Park the radios a mile or two apart. The numbers are the point — carts, active, lead, tail, span.
  • ⭐ Use TOPO+, not satellite. Topo hides the parking lots and cul-de-sacs that gave the last one away.

Every other screenshot is real Utah trail data, so the listing as a whole reads correctly. This is one panel out of eight.

Still to decide

  • Price. The listing must not promise what is not implemented. ⭐ If early access is genuinely free, say so — it is a strong line and it sets an expectation you can charge against later.
  • Does the radio get named? Tracking needs a Meshtastic radio, and the automated setup is T1000-E only. Saying so costs some installs and prevents every one-star that opens "I could not get tracking to work." ⚠ Recommend a plain line near the mesh paragraph.
  • Coverage. Trail data is per-state and the app is built around Utah riding. If a rider in Michigan installs it and finds thin data, that is a bad review. Worth a line naming which states have good coverage.

⛔ Play Store targeting — the title is costing you

The app title is the highest-weighted keyword field in Play search. It is 30 characters and "GroupTrack" uses 10. The other 20 are the most valuable text in the entire listing and they are currently empty.

Nobody searches for "GroupTrack" — they do not know it exists. They search for what they want:

Candidate titleCharsPicks up
GroupTrack: Offroad Trail Maps30 offroad, trail, maps — the three highest-volume terms
GroupTrack — UTV Trail Maps27 UTV specifically; narrower, far less competition
GroupTrack: OHV Trails Offline30 leads on offline, the durable differentiator

⭐ Recommended: "GroupTrack: Offroad Trail Maps". A rider searching "offroad trail maps" is exactly your buyer, and the brand still comes first.

Where the keywords actually go

Play indexes the title, the short description and the full description — there is no separate keyword field. The vocabulary has to be worked into prose that still reads naturally. ⚠ Stuffing is penalised and reads badly to a human.

The Quick Start already does this correctly — "side-by-side, UTV, ATV, dirt bike and Jeep" is five search terms in one honest sentence, which is why it now opens the description.

Terms worth appearing at least once, naturally: offroad · off-road · trail maps · offline maps · UTV · side-by-side · ATV · OHV · dirt bike · Jeep · route planner · GPS tracking · topo.

Category and tags

  • Category: Maps & Navigation. Not Sports — that is where fitness trackers live and your buyer is not browsing there. ⚠ Not Travel & Local either; that is restaurants and hotels.
  • Tags — Play allows up to five, set in Store settings. Navigation, Maps, Outdoors, Off-road, Trip Planning. These drive the "similar apps" placement, which is how a rider looking at onX finds you.

⭐ The comparison you want to be in

A rider who has installed onX Offroad or Gaia GPS and found them wanting is your best prospect — they already know they need this kind of app. Tags and category are what put you in those listings' "similar apps" row, so they are worth more attention than they usually get.

Acquisition

The Facebook groups are the whole channel

Eight to ten Utah UTV groups is worth more than anything else available, and they punish anything that reads as an ad.

⭐ Post a result, not a product. A screenshot of a route through ground people know, with the app incidental — "found this loop off Jockey Road, 77 miles, eight summits." Let somebody ask what it is. The routes-compared screen does this job on its own.

The claim nobody can match

"2,400 miles of Utah trail that onX does not show." True, checkable, and it is the only line in this listing a competitor cannot copy. Worth using somewhere prominent.

Jamborees

Rattlesnake, Red Rim, Yankee Meadows and Broken Ridge all appear in your own track names, so you are already in those circles. A jamboree is a hundred riders with the same problem standing in one parking lot.

⚠ One thing to fix first

The 30-minute first download is the biggest install-to-uninstall risk in the product. It is in the description above, but it also belongs in the first sentence of any Facebook post — a rider who starts it on mobile data at a trailhead is a lost user and a bad review.
