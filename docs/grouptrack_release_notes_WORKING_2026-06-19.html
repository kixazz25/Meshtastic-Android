GroupTrack V2.5 — Release Notes
What's new · Field Test Release · Updated 2026-06-18 · grouptrack.org
⚠ Updating from an earlier version? Read this first.

Install as an UPDATE — do NOT uninstall the old version and install this as a new install. Let the app update in place through the Play Store. Uninstalling and then clean-installing will break the installation, and you will have to reach out for help to recover it.

After the update, on first launch the trail/track database is rebuilt. So:

  • Re-import your trails — open Work with Artifacts → Import Trails and import UGRC Utah Trails again (brings back trails + trailhead waypoints).
  • Your recorded tracks come back automatically from your saved GPX files on launch. If any track is missing, open the track import screen and tap RESYNC TRACKS.
  • Your GPX track files and downloaded map tiles are not affected by the update.

If you already did a clean install and it’s broken: the fix is to remove the two GroupTrack database files from /sdcard/Documents/GroupTrack/ before relaunching — contact the organizer for help, as this is a manual step.

GroupTrack V2.5

Release Notes

May 2026 | grouptrack.org

⚠️ IMPORTANT CHANGE ⚠️

Trail data is no longer included with the app.

In V2.4, GroupTrack included pre-loaded trail overlays for southern Utah. In V2.5, trail data is no longer bundled with the app. Instead, you select and import trail data for the regions where you ride. This gives you access to trail databases across multiple states and agencies instead of a single fixed area.

Before your first ride with V2.5, you must import trail data for your riding area. This is a simple one-time process that takes a few minutes on WiFi. Once imported, trail data is permanent on your device and displays automatically.

How to import trails: Open the Planning Map, tap WORK WITH ARTIFACTS, tap IMPORT TRAILS, and follow the step-by-step guide. Choose Import by Source (select a land agency) or Import by Area (draw a box on the map). Full walkthrough in the Trail Import Guide, Chapter 11 of the V2.5 User Manual.

Latest in this build (June 18)

Find-and-fit, help on every map, and clearer track arrows:

  • Fit a found artifact to your map — open any artifact's detail card and tap FIT. The map flies to that item and frames it, the item is selected and drawn on its own, and everything else is turned off so you see just the one you picked. Works for trails, tracks, waypoints, and routes, on both the Convoy Map and the Planning Map. (This is the find → detail → fit flow finishing the loop: search a name, open the detail, tap FIT.)
  • Help (?) is now on the Convoy Map too — the ? button that opens the release notes and the user manual, already on the Planning Map, is now on the Convoy Map as well. Both maps open the same documents, so help is one tap away wherever you are.
  • Clearer direction arrows on tracks — the direction-of-travel arrows on displayed tracks are now evenly spaced on screen and stay readable as you zoom, instead of thinning out along long tracks. They're drawn in the same neon green as the track. (This replaces the earlier behavior where a long track could show almost no arrows when you zoomed in.)

    • 📖 Manual Reference: Chapter 6 (Work With Artifacts — detail & FIT), Chapter 2 (Convoy Map — Help), Chapter 9 (Track Display)

    Latest in this build (June 13)

    Find any artifact by name, and see its full details:

  • Search artifacts by name — in Work with Artifacts, pick a type (Trail, Track, Waypoint, or Route), type a name, and press Enter to find matches across your whole database, not just what's on screen. Case-insensitive, matches partial names. Same-named segments (e.g. the many "Jordan River Trail" sections) are numbered so you can tell them apart.
  • Artifact detail card — tap a search result (or an item in the select/edit list) to open its detail: the actions available for that type on the left, and on the right the artifact's information — for trails this includes designated uses, surface type, classification, county, and managing agency. Less-used technical fields (coordinates, dates, hash) tuck under a "technical" toggle so the useful information leads.
  • Alternate names — where a trail has alternate names on record, they're shown on the detail card.

    • 📖 Manual Reference: Chapter 4 / 6 (Work With Artifacts — Search & Detail)

    Latest in this build (June 11)

    Map display polish and stability fixes in the most recent field-test build:

  • Waypoints now show as teardrop pins — the pin's point marks the exact spot you dropped it, instead of a centered circle. Each waypoint type keeps its own color and icon.
  • Direction arrows on displayed tracks — turn Tracks on and your recorded tracks show direction-of-travel arrows along the line, so you can read which way a track runs at a glance (handy for telling a single looping track from two stacked ones). (Spacing and readability were improved in the June 18 build.)
  • In-progress route list fixed — the route picker now reflects what's actually saved on your device (no more stale "ghost" entries), and shows a clear "No in-progress routes yet" message when empty.
  • Startup stability — resolved two startup freezes (storage-permission and tile-cache scan) that could cause the app to hang on launch.

    • 📖 Manual Reference: Chapter 2 (Convoy Map — Waypoints), Chapter 9 (Track Display), Chapter 4 (Creating a Route)

    New Features in V2.5

    Spatial Trail Database

    Trails are now powered by an on-device spatial database with GeoPackage architecture. Trail data from USFS, BLM, state parks, and other public land agencies is downloaded, indexed, and displayed dynamically based on your map viewport. Only trails visible on your screen are loaded, delivering fast performance even with tens of thousands of trails.

  • Viewport-aware lazy loading replaces full-dataset loading from V2.4
  • Bounding box spatial indexing for millisecond query response
  • Trails display at zoom level 8 and above
  • Trail data persists permanently on device across app updates
  • Support for multiple trail sources and agencies simultaneously

    • 📖 Manual Reference: Chapter 6 (Work With Artifacts), Chapter 9A (Trail Display), Chapter 11 (Trail Import Guide)

    Track Spatial Display

    Your recorded GPS tracks are now stored in the spatial database alongside trails. Tracks from your my_tracks/ library are automatically synced into the database on app launch. Display uses the same viewport-aware lazy loading as trails, providing instant rendering at any zoom level.

  • Automatic GPX-to-spatial-database sync on app start
  • Dashed neon green lines (#39FF14) distinguish tracks from trails
  • Viewport-aware loading for instant pan/zoom response
  • Bounding box indexing for fast track queries
  • Tracks and trails can display simultaneously without performance impact

    • 📖 Manual Reference: Chapter 9 (Track Display)

    Work With Artifacts Panel

    The V2.4 Display Panel is replaced by the Work with Artifacts panel, a comprehensive interface for managing all spatial data types. The panel provides display toggles for Trails, Tracks, Waypoints, and Routes, plus import buttons for downloading new data.

  • Four artifact types with independent display toggles
  • Artifact counts shown per type
  • IMPORT TRAILS button for trail source downloads
  • IMPORT ARTIFACTS button for GPX file imports
  • Display-only mode on Convoy Map, full mode on Planning Map

    • 📖 Manual Reference: Chapter 6 (Work With Artifacts)

    Trail Import System

    Import trail data from public land management agencies using two methods: by Source (select a specific agency dataset) or by Area (draw a bounding box and get suggested sources). Trail import is a one-time process per region.

  • Import by Source: browse and select from agency trail catalogs
  • Import by Area: draw a rectangle, see sources with overlap percentages
  • Validation screen before download with trail counts and file sizes
  • Progress tracking during import
  • Duplicate detection prevents redundant trail imports

    • 📖 Manual Reference: Chapter 11 (Trail Import Guide)

    Download Queue Management

    V2.5 adds management controls to the tile download queue. Hold, resume, and cancel operations give you full control over background downloads. Individual queue entries can be cancelled without affecting other downloads.

  • Hold Queue: pause all processing, resume when ready
  • Cancel Entry: stop and remove a single download
  • Cancel All: clear the entire queue at once
  • Downloaded tiles are preserved when cancelling partial downloads

    • 📖 Manual Reference: Chapter 10 (Download Queue)

    Expanded Tile Sources

    The tile source catalog expands from 3 to 16 sources across 6 categories: Hybrid, Satellite, Topographic, Street, Terrain, and Outdoor. Any source can be assigned to any of the three toolbar slots (SAT/TOPO/TOPO+). Premium sources requiring API keys are supported with built-in key management.

  • 16 tile sources from Esri, OpenTopo, Thunderforest, and others
  • Flexible slot assignment: any source in any toolbar position
  • API key management for premium sources (stored locally)
  • Source change triggers intelligent tile refresh in background

    • 📖 Manual Reference: Chapter 12 (Tile Sources), Settings > Map Sources

    Trail Color Classification

    Trails display in classification-based colors so you can identify trail types at a glance while riding. OHV trails stand out in blue with heavier line weight. Non-motorized trails show in distinct colors so you can tell which trails are open to your vehicle.

  • Blue: OHV and road-concurrent trails (heaviest line weight)
  • Orange: Hiking and biking multi-use trails
  • Yellow: Hiking-only trails
  • Purple: Biking-only trails
  • Cyan: Unclassified or paved shared-use trails

    • 📖 Manual Reference: Chapter 9A (Trail Display)
