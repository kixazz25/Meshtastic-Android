GroupTrack V2.6 — Release Notes
What's new · Field Test Release · Rel 2.6e · Updated 2026-07-28 · grouptrack.org
⚠ IMPORTANT — read this before you proceed

Install as an UPDATE — do NOT uninstall the old version and install this as a new install. Let the app update in place through the Play Store.

V2.6 changes how offline map tiles are stored — to a single on-device spatial database per map type, which makes stored maps roughly half the size (field-measured ~55% smaller for satellite imagery). Before your maps will work, the old tiles must be cleared once and re-downloaded in the new format. Do this first:

  1. Install X-plore. In the Google Play Store, search “X-plore File Manager” and install it (free). X-plore shows its progress the whole time — a live count of folders remaining and the file names as they clear — so you always know it's working.
  2. Delete the old tiles folder. In X-plore, go to Internal storage → Documents → GroupTrack → maps and delete the maps folder. If you've downloaded a lot of maps this can take a while — let it run; the on-screen count shows it's actively working. Keep the device plugged in for a large cleanup.
  3. Re-download your maps. Back in GroupTrack, download the areas you need again (by Area, or from a track or route). They're saved in the new format at roughly half the previous size.

Your tracks and routes are not affected — this clears only downloaded map tiles (the imagery/topo backgrounds). Recorded tracks, imported tracks, and routes are stored separately and untouched.

This is a one-time step. Once your maps are re-downloaded in the new format, future updates won't need it.

New in this release · Rel 2.6e · 2026-08-03

Planning map — fit to an artifact

Choosing FIT on a track, trail, route or waypoint now reliably centres and frames it on the planning map. Previously the map could stay exactly where it was, showing nothing, and the only way to recover was to switch to the convoy map and come back.

Tapping the map while planning

Tapping the planning map no longer opens a list of nearby artifacts. That list could name trails that were not being shown on screen — including types you had switched off — which made it look as though hidden trails had come back.

To look at an artifact while building a route, switch the panel from Route to Artifact. Point-adding stops, so you can tap the map and read a popup without dropping a vertex. Switch back to Route to carry on. This is now the way to inspect artifacts mid-route, and it is documented in the manual with screenshots.

The convoy map is unchanged — tapping there works exactly as before.

Manual

The route creation section has been rebuilt with twelve screenshots taken from this build, covering the whole workflow: the panel that lists unfinished routes, naming an auto-saved route, placing points, and saving.

Two things are documented for the first time:

  • Snap-2 — placing your second point at an intersection lets the route follow the trail itself rather than a straight line between taps, and on a trail with no intersections a single further point can carry the route a long way along it.
  • The Artifact / Route toggle — how to inspect what is on the map without adding route points.

Smaller install

Twenty-three leftover working files had been shipping inside the app since early July. They are gone. Nothing you use is affected — they were never read.

Previously · Rel 2.6e · 2026-08-02

Satellite maps

Google Hybrid works again — satellite imagery with roads and place names built in
Google Hybrid had stopped returning any map tiles and looked like a source that had been withdrawn. It turned out to be a fault in how the app built the web address for each tile, so every request went nowhere. It now works both online and for downloads. Unlike the plain satellite view, Google Hybrid already has road lines and place names printed into the image, so you get them without a separate labels layer.

Planning routes

One panel now handles everything when you tap +ROUTE
Starting a route used to walk you through up to three separate pop-ups before you could do anything. There is now a single panel listing every unfinished route with the date you started it and how many points it has, with Continue, Rename and Delete on each one, plus Plan a New Route and Cancel at the bottom.
An unfinished route left by a crash no longer blocks you silently
If the app closes while you are drawing, the part-finished route is kept under a placeholder name. When you enter the planning map you now get a short message telling you how many unfinished routes you have. Starting a brand-new route is blocked until you either give that one a proper name or delete it — and the message says so, rather than simply doing nothing.
Cancelling out of route planning really cancels
Previously, tapping +ROUTE and then backing out could leave the map still in point-placing mode, so the next tap dropped a route point instead of opening a trail. Cancelling — or tapping outside the panel — now switches it off properly.
Tapping while drawing a route no longer opens trail information
While you are placing points, a tap sometimes also popped up details for whatever was underneath. Taps now go to the route only.
Map taps are more precise
A tap used to look about 200 metres out for anything nearby, which meant it often found something on the far side of your own position marker. That is now about 100 metres, so what you get is what you aimed at.

Off-grid

Your position shows on the map with no internet connection
Starting the app out of signal could leave you with a blank screen and no vehicle marker, even though the app knew where you were and was recording your track correctly. The marker and your recorded track now draw as they should. If you are somewhere you have not downloaded maps for, the background will still be empty — there is nothing stored to draw — but your position appears on top of it.

Previously · Rel 2.6e · 2026-07-30

Tapping the map

Tapping a trail or a track now opens its details reliably
Tapping the planning map has been unreliable — sometimes a name appeared, more often several taps were needed and nothing happened. That is fixed. A single tap on a trail or a track opens its details, and a tap on open ground lists whatever is nearby.
Benefit: the map answers the first time you tap it.
Manual: Planning map
Long press always drops a waypoint
Press and hold anywhere on the planning map to place a waypoint. This now works in every situation, including while you are building a route — previously it was blocked whenever route building was open.
Benefit: you can mark something the moment you notice it, without leaving what you were doing.
Manual: Planning map

Building routes

Routes snap to trails, and draw straight lines everywhere else
When you tap while building a route, the point attaches to a trail if there is one under your finger. Recorded tracks are no longer used as snap targets — they still draw on the map and you can still tap them for details, but a route drawn across open ground now runs straight between your points instead of following someone's recording.
Benefit: routes follow the trail network, and a track you rode once does not pull your route off course.
Manual: Planning map
Snapping now reaches as far as tapping does
Snapping used to work within a fixed distance on the ground, while tapping worked within a distance on the screen — so when you zoomed out, a tap could show a trail's name and still not attach to it. The two now match at every zoom level.
Benefit: you can draw a route zoomed out over a wide area, not just zoomed right in.
Manual: Planning map

OpenStreetMap trail data

Unpaved public roads now import alongside the trails
The import now includes the bottom rung of the public road network — the unclassified roads that are often dirt, gravel or stone and are exactly what a lot of riding actually follows. For Utah that added about 14,500 routes on top of what was already there.
Benefit: far fewer places where you can see a road on the satellite image but there is nothing to snap to.
Manual: Importing OSM data
The downloaded file is removed as soon as it has been used
The Geofabrik download is several hundred megabytes and is only needed until the trail database has been built from it. It is now deleted the moment the extract succeeds, rather than waiting for cleanup.
Benefit: the largest file of the set stops occupying your device as early as possible.
Manual: Importing OSM data

OpenStreetMap trail data

Import OpenStreetMap trails — a whole state, or just the area you ride
GroupTrack can now take a free OpenStreetMap extract and turn it into trail data held on your own device. It fills in ground the official trail sources do not cover, and once imported it works entirely offline. You prepare one state at a time, then import either the whole state or just an area you draw on the map — a corner of Arizona or a stretch of Nevada. Open the planning map, tap WORK WITH ARTIFACTS, then IMPORT OSM DATA.
Benefit: far more trail coverage in areas where government data is thin or missing.
Manual: Work with Artifacts
Four steps, each showing its own progress
1 — Download. Tap OPEN GEOFABRIK, choose your state, and download the file in your browser. 2 — Extract. Come back to GroupTrack and tap the button under step 1. The app finds your download and builds a compact trail database from it. 3 — Import. Add the trails to your map — the whole state, or an area you draw. 4 — Clean up. Remove the working files and keep a record of what was imported.
Benefit: each step tells you what it is doing, how far along it is, and roughly how long is left — and ticks green when it finishes. Leave the app at any point and come back: you are returned to the next step, not to the beginning.
Manual: Importing OSM data
Place names, peaks and springs come with it
Alongside trails, the import collects named places, mountain peaks, springs, caves and other natural features. These are the landmarks that matter once you are past the edge of the towns.
Benefit: recognisable names on the map in country where satellite labels have run out.
Manual: Importing OSM data
Cancel at any point
Downloaded the wrong state, or changed your mind part-way through? CANCEL AND DELETE DOWNLOAD at the bottom of the panel stops whatever is running and clears the working files.
Benefit: a wrong download costs you a minute, not a stuck screen you cannot get out of.
Manual: Importing OSM data

Before you start

Check you have room
A state download is large — typically 300–600 MB, and roughly double that while the data is being unpacked. GroupTrack checks before it begins and tells you if there is not enough space. Working files are kept in Documents → GroupTrack → osm where you can see them yourself.
Benefit: no half-finished import that fails part-way through and leaves you guessing.
Manual: Importing OSM data
GroupTrack needs All files access
The app needs All files access to see your browser downloads. If you ever clear the app’s storage, Android switches this off silently and the import will report that it cannot find your download. Turn it back on under Settings → Apps → Special app access → All files access.
Benefit: you will know why the import cannot see a file that is plainly sitting in your Downloads folder.
Manual: Permissions

Adding the trails to your map

Choose the whole state, or draw the area you ride
Step 3 asks how much of the state you want. FULL STATE takes every trail in the extract. SELECTED AREA closes the panel, moves the map to cover your state with the drawing tools open, and imports only what is inside the box you draw. The IMPORT button stays greyed out until you have chosen, so you cannot start without saying what you want.
Benefit: a state download can be turned into exactly the ground you are riding, instead of everything.
Manual: Importing OSM data
You are told how many trails before anything is added
The confirmation names the count and the area — for Utah, 89,536 trails for the whole state. Place names and natural features are always imported for the whole state even when you have drawn an area, because there are only a few thousand of them and they are useful everywhere. There is no undo, so the count is there to be read.
Benefit: no surprises about the size of what you just started.
Manual: Importing OSM data
The import runs in the background
Importing a whole state takes a few minutes. It runs with a notification, so you can lock the screen or use another app. Come back to the panel and it re-attaches to the running import rather than looking as though nothing happened.
Benefit: you are not held on one screen watching a bar.
Manual: Importing OSM data
Importing the same ground twice is safe
When it finishes you get a summary: how many trails were added, how many matched something you already had and were recorded as another name for it, and how many were identical to trails already on your map so nothing was added. Import a state you already have and it will report almost everything as identical and add nothing.
Benefit: you will never end up with two copies of the same trail.
Manual: Importing OSM data

Cleaning up

Step 4 turns green once an import has landed
Cleanup is not automatic — it waits for you to choose it. The downloaded file has already been removed by this point; cleanup clears what is left, the unpacked data and the trail database built from it. It keeps a small record of what you downloaded, what was extracted and exactly what each import added, written before anything is deleted.
Benefit: the large working files do not sit on your device once they have done their job.
Manual: Importing OSM data
Re-importing a state means downloading it again
Because the working files are removed, importing that state again later — another area, or the whole state — starts with a fresh download from Geofabrik. That is usually about three minutes on Wi-Fi. Importing ground you already have is safe: it reports almost everything as already present and adds nothing, so you never end up with two copies of a trail.
Benefit: nothing large is kept on the chance you might want it.

Previously · Rel 2.6d · 2026-07-24

Everything below is new since Rel 2.6c.

Saving maps for a trail

Saving maps from a track now follows the trail instead of boxing it in
When you save maps for a track, GroupTrack now downloads a corridor along the trail itself — the path plus about half a mile either side — instead of the whole rectangle that encloses the track. A long trail that wanders across a wide area used to pull down everything inside that rectangle, most of which is ground nobody rides.
Benefit: dramatically smaller downloads for the same trail. On a ~100-mile test track that doubles back on itself, the download went from over a million tiles to about 107,000 — roughly a 90% reduction, and hours off the wait. The gain is largest on long, winding trails; a short compact loop will see less difference, because its rectangle was already close to the trail.
Manual: Offline Maps & Downloads
Importing tracks saves maps the same way
When you import tracks with the “save maps” option turned on, each track now queues a corridor download instead of a rectangle. Each track queues one job per map source, so importing ten tracks with three sources selected produces thirty jobs in the queue — that is normal, and each one is far smaller than before.
Benefit: bulk imports no longer queue enormous rectangles of terrain you will never ride.
Manual: Offline Maps & Downloads
A brief pause before a large import starts downloading
GroupTrack now works out the exact corridor for each track before queueing it, so a large import spends a moment calculating before the first tile arrives.
This is expected, not a freeze — that calculation is what makes the smaller download possible.

The downloads panel — rebuilt

The downloads indicator now opens a full download manager
Tapping the downloads indicator no longer half-expands a small popup — it opens the download manager itself, where everything about the queue lives. The indicator stays compact until you want it.
Benefit: one tap gets you to the controls instead of a summary you then have to leave.
Manual: Offline Maps & Downloads
The queue list scrolls through every job
The queue list previously stopped part-way down and would not scroll further, so with a long queue most jobs simply could not be reached. The list has been rebuilt and now scrolls through the whole queue however many jobs are waiting.
Benefit: with a couple of hundred jobs queued you can see and reach all of them.
Manual: Offline Maps & Downloads
A summary that shows what is running, what is waiting, and how long it will take
Three lines at the top of the manager: what is downloading right now and its tile count, how many jobs are still queued and their total tiles, and an estimated time remaining with a projected finish time. The queued tile total is new — the old summary only counted the jobs actually running, so a large queue looked far smaller than it was. The time estimate is worked out from how fast your own recent downloads actually completed rather than from a fixed guess, and it appears once a couple of downloads have finished on this version.
Benefit: you can tell at a glance whether a queue finishes overnight or needs trimming before you leave.
Manual: Offline Maps & Downloads
Every job says what kind it is and when it will run
Each row in the queue now shows the job’s type and its place in the running order — for example an area download, a trail corridor, a map-source refresh, or a tile deletion — alongside the source it is fetching and its progress.
Benefit: the running order is visible instead of looking arbitrary, so you can see why a particular job is waiting.
Manual: Offline Maps & Downloads
Filter the queue by job type — and act only on what you filtered
Filter buttons split the queue by kind: all jobs, areas you drew, trail corridors, map-source refreshes, and deletions. The filter also controls the Cancel and Clear buttons, so those act only on the jobs currently shown. Filter to corridors, cancel, and your area downloads are left alone.
Benefit: you can clear out one kind of job without disturbing the rest.
Manual: Offline Maps & Downloads
Cancel and Clear say exactly what they will do, and ask first
The buttons name their scope and count — “CANCEL CORRIDOR (54)” when a filter is active, “CANCEL ALL (215)” when it is not — and always ask for confirmation before doing anything, with the same scope and count repeated in the question.
Benefit: nothing destructive happens on a single tap, and you always know whether you are clearing a few jobs or all of them.
Manual: Offline Maps & Downloads
Move a download to the front of the queue
Each waiting job has a ▲ button that moves it ahead of the others. Jobs otherwise run in a sensible order: tile deletions first, then trail corridors, then drawn areas, then map-source refreshes. Promotion applies to the row you tap — if a track queued three jobs, one per map source, promote each of the three.
Benefit: the map you need for tomorrow’s ride does not sit behind a large area download.
Manual: Offline Maps & Downloads

Freeing up space

Remove downloaded maps for an area
Draw an area on the planning map, open the downloads panel, tick Remove Tiles and press the red button. GroupTrack asks you to confirm, then removes every downloaded map tile inside that area — across all map sources, including the road and place-name layers that sit on top of satellite imagery. The removal runs as a job in the download queue like any other, so you can watch it and carry on using the app.
Benefit: you can clear out map data for places you have finished riding without wiping everything and starting again. Useful when the device is filling up and you only want back the space taken by one region.
Manual: Offline Maps & Downloads
Removal cannot be undone, so it asks first
The confirmation names the area and gives an upper bound on how many tiles will go. Nothing is removed until you confirm.
Benefit: a mis-tap cannot cost you hours of downloading.
Storage does not drop immediately after a removal
The tiles are gone and the space inside GroupTrack’s map files is free for new downloads to reuse — but the files themselves keep their current size on disk until they are compacted. Compacting is not automatic yet.
This is expected. New map downloads will fill the freed space rather than growing the files further.
Large areas take a while, and you can watch or stop them
Removing map data from a large area is a lot of work and can run for several minutes. Progress updates steadily as it goes, and Cancel takes effect promptly if you change your mind.
Benefit: a big removal behaves like any other queued job — visible, and yours to stop.

Fixes

Download options are reachable on the convoy map in portrait
On the convoy map, the download options box — where you pick map sources and choose whether to replace existing tiles — sat too low and ran off the bottom of the screen, so its buttons could only be reached by turning the phone sideways. It now opens centred on the screen, matching the planning map, and can be dismissed with the back button.
Benefit: you no longer have to rotate the device to start a download from the convoy map.
Map source choices are still lost if you rotate the device
If you turn the phone while the download options box is open, the ticked map sources reset.
Known limitation in this build. You should no longer need to rotate to reach the buttons, so pick your sources and confirm without turning the device.

Previously · Rel 2.6c · 2026-07-22

Everything below is new since the 2026-07-06 build.

Offline maps & downloads

Smaller map storage — the spatial-database tile store
Offline map tiles now live in a single spatial database per map type instead of millions of separate files, compressed and de-duplicated in one efficient container. See the upgrade note at the top — existing tiles must be cleared once.
Benefit: about half the storage for your offline maps (field-measured ~55% smaller for satellite), with no visible quality loss at normal zoom.
Manual: Offline Maps & Downloads
Much faster map downloads
Map tiles are now fetched in parallel instead of one at a time. A large multi-source area (satellite plus topo layers) that could previously take many hours now completes in roughly an hour and a half in field testing. If a map server is briefly busy or returns a temporary error, the app backs off and retries that tile instead of skipping it.
Benefit: several times faster downloads, and they finish more complete.
Manual: Offline Maps & Downloads
Big downloads split into balanced pieces
A large download area is now divided into evenly sized pieces sized off the real tile count, and every map source uses the same grid so their pieces line up. Two pieces download at once, each fetching several tiles in parallel.
Benefit: no single oversized job stalls the queue — large areas move steadily instead of sitting on one huge chunk.
Manual: Offline Maps & Downloads
Changing a map source re-downloads in balanced pieces, with progress
When you switch a map slot to a different source, the refresh is now split the same way an area download is, instead of a fixed grid. Each piece also reports its real tile count, so the queue shows a total and a progress percentage while it works.
Benefit: source changes download faster and you can see exactly how far along they are.
Manual: Map Sources
Full-detail topo downloads
The TOPO and TOPO+ layers now download their full available zoom detail (a previous internal limit has been removed). The USGS TOPO+ source still has no imagery above about zoom 15 — that is a source limitation, unchanged.
Benefit: sharper topo maps offline where the source supports it.
Manual: Offline Maps & Downloads
Offline map labels fixed
Road and place-name labels on the satellite map now stay visible after you go offline and when you switch map areas. Previously they could vanish on the first offline switch and not come back.
Benefit: reliable labels wherever you are, on or off the grid.
Download maps from the main map
You can now start a map-area download for a track straight from its detail panel on the main convoy map — the same source picker and replace option you get on the planning map. Area, track and import downloads all go through the same download panel now.
Benefit: grab offline maps for a track without leaving the convoy view, and the same controls everywhere.
Manual: Offline Maps & Downloads
Download queue survives interruptions
If the app was closed or killed while it was saving the download queue, the queue file could be left damaged — and the app would then keep failing to load it on every later start. The queue is now written safely, and a damaged file is set aside automatically so the app starts clean.
Benefit: an interrupted download can't leave your queue in a state you'd have to clear app data to escape.

Building routes

Route building saves as you go
An in-progress route now saves automatically as you place each point, under a working name. There is no "name your route" prompt when you start — you name it when you save. Undo removes the last point and the saved copy updates to match.
Benefit: a route in progress is never lost, even if the app closes unexpectedly.
Manual: Routes
Naming a route is now required when you save
When you save a route, a name is required. The field starts blank for an auto-saved route — type a name before saving. Save as a completed route (needs 2 or more points), or keep it as a named work-in-progress you can resume later.
Benefit: your saved routes carry real names instead of all sharing the working one.
Manual: Routes
Recovering a route after an unexpected close
If the app closes unexpectedly (crash, force-close, battery) while a route is in progress, the next time you open the Planning map GroupTrack shows a notice: a route was left open from your last session. Tap OK, then +ROUTE → In Progress and pick the auto-saved route to carry on. A normal route close or save does not trigger the notice.
Benefit: an interrupted route is still there when you come back, and the app tells you how to get to it.
Manual: Routes
A cleaner route-building mode
Starting a route now puts the map straight into route mode, with a Route / Artifact switch so you can choose whether a tap adds a route point or works with artifacts. While you are placing route points, tapping a track no longer opens its details and no waypoint is dropped by mistake. Backing out of an in-progress route asks you to save or discard first.
Benefit: fewer stray taps and accidental waypoints while building, and no route lost by pressing Back.
Manual: Routes

Tracks & artifacts

Delete and rename now work
Deleting or renaming a track, route or waypoint from its detail panel had no effect — the item stayed exactly as it was. Both now work correctly on the convoy map and the planning map, and deleting a track removes it everywhere it was stored, including its recorded file.
Benefit: you can clean up and correct your saved artifacts instead of watching them pile up.
Manual: Work with Artifacts
Track import tells you what it is doing
Importing a batch of tracks used to leave you looking at a blank screen for as long as an hour with no sign of progress. The import now reports track by track as it works through them, so you can see each one being processed and how far along the batch is.
Benefit: you know the import is running and roughly how much is left, instead of wondering whether the app has hung.
Manual: Importing

Benefit: read which way a track runs at a glance, even on long tracks.
Manual: Tracks
Each map remembers where you left it
The Convoy and Planning maps each restore their own last view and what you had selected, independently of each other.
Benefit: pick up exactly where you were, on the right map.
Manual: Maps
Build routes that follow real trails
Tap to drop points and each one snaps to the nearest trail or track; tap away from a trail to place a free point. Name it and save (early access).
Benefit: lay out a route along established paths in seconds.
Manual: Creating a Route
Bring your own trail data
Trail data is no longer bundled with the app. You import the regions where you ride — by Source (pick a land agency) or by Area (draw a box). One-time, a few minutes on WiFi; it’s permanent on your device after that.
Benefit: trails for the places you actually ride, across many states and agencies.
Manual: Trail Sources
Faster maps with the spatial database
Trails and tracks load only for the part of the map you’re looking at, from an on-device spatial database. Tracks sync in automatically from your GPX files on launch.
Benefit: instant pan and zoom even with tens of thousands of trails.
Manual: Trail & track display
Work with Artifacts, now icon-driven
A right-edge icon column on both maps gives you search, Work with Artifacts, and help. The panel opens expanded and collapses closed — the old always-on bar is gone.
Benefit: more map, controls one tap away when you want them.
Manual: The right-edge icon column
More map sources, and download controls
16 tile sources (Google, Esri, USGS, OpenTopo, OSM, Thunderforest, CartoDB) assignable to your SAT / TOPO / TOPO+ buttons, with API-key management. The download queue supports hold, resume, cancel, and a settable number of simultaneous downloads.
Benefit: the basemaps you prefer, and control over big offline downloads.
