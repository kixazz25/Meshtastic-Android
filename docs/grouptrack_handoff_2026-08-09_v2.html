⭐⭐ THE IDEA WORTH KEEPING — A REBUILD MANIFEST

Fred, 08-09: "save downloads in queue and flag source area or corr. if we lose data we can recreate by resubmitting areas and corridors that have been completed — kind of a rebuild my map script." And: "if you store it on Google Drive it can be used to seed another device." And: "and a DR tool."

Keep completed download jobs — scope and kind — and you have a MANIFEST of how the store was built. Every corridor by geom hash, every area by bbox, each tagged. Not a log, a recipe.

What it gives:

  • DR. Two data-loss events in nine days, both ending in manual reconstruction. With a manifest: reinstall, pull, resubmit, walk away. ⚠ Covers TILES only — tracks and trails are captured data and need real backup.
  • Device seeding. A manifest is kilobytes; 14 GB of tiles is not. New phone: install, pull, rebuild. A seed, not a transfer.
  • Coverage sharing. A ride leader builds the coverage for a trip and shares the recipe. ⭐ The route-import goal generalised to whole stores.
  • It dissolves the delete ordering constraint. Area scope currently must be inferred from what survives on disk, which is WHY corridors must be deleted first. A manifest records what was requested, so scope no longer comes from disk.

⚠ The pieces mostly exist — completed entries already carry geomHash, bbox, slot and tile counts. Missing: an explicit kind flag, and CLEAR currently destroys them. ⭐ The hide-not-delete change already designed for the yardstick serves both.

⚠ It must not live in download_queue.json — that file quarantines to .corrupt on a bad parse and has boot-looped the app. A DR tool cannot share fate with the thing it protects. ⭐ Off-device, which is what the Drive idea already does.

⚠ A shared manifest carries where you ride. Be deliberate before it becomes a sharing feature.

⚠⚠ WHY TILE-BY-TILE REFRESH WAS REJECTED (considered and killed tonight): a single job over 1.28M tiles is one unit with no restart point. Fred: "one two week job with no restart for failsafe. not buying it." The corridors and areas are not just scope — they are the FAILURE BOUNDARIES that make a multi-day job survivable. Chunking by count (~40k per job) would work, but the payload must be a re-queryable range, not a tile list.

⚠ And the MBTiles metadata carries nothing useful — name, format, scheme. Checked day one. There is no record of how any tile got there, which is the same gap behind the coverage-confidence question. It is our file; we can add rows.
