▾ How are trails recommended?

The app scores a ride on what it passes and how much of it you ride twice. Rides that reach named features — springs, canyons, overlooks, ruins, mines — score higher than rides that just cover ground.

Riding the same trail back the way you came counts against a ride, so loops and new ground are favoured over out-and-backs.

Distance is a budget, not a goal. A ride that hits your mileage but passes nothing interesting scores below a shorter one that does.

It only builds on trails that are actually connected to each other in the map data. Some trails in your area are recorded as isolated pieces with nothing joining them, and those cannot be used until better data exists.

Mode derivation

Pins droppedMode sent to the search
Trailhead onlyAI_MODE_EXPLORE · loop
Trailhead + finishAI_MODE_EXPLORE · point to point
Trailhead + any includesAI_MODE_INCLUDE
Trailhead + finish + includesAI_MODE_INCLUDE · point to point

Nothing ambiguous, nothing to ask. The rider never sees the words Explore or Assist — they drop pins and the app does the right thing.

The pin panel

One component, three prompts. Drop mechanic, snap and map behaviour are identical; only the prompt, the counter and the button labels change.

StepPromptPinsExit
1 TrailheadTap where the ride startsOne, replaces No skip — the gate
2 FinishTap where the ride ends, or skip for a loopOne, replaces SKIP → NEXT once set
3 IncludesTap places you want the ride to pass throughUp to 10 SKIP → DONE once set

BACK returns to the prior step with its pin still shown and replaceable. Tapping an existing include pin removes it.

Step 3's prompt now carries feature discovery. With the mode derived, a rider who only ever drops a trailhead would never learn include points exist — except that this step asks every single time, whether they use it or not.

That makes its wording load-bearing in a way it wasn't when a radio button announced the feature.

Rendering

  • Trailhead — green, labelled
  • Finish — amber, labelled
  • Includes — blue, numbered 1–10

Three symbols means a rider looking at the map after step 3 sees the whole shape of what they asked for, without reading anything.

The two twisties

TwistyDefaultWhy
How does this work?Open Sets the expectation that pins are coming and that nothing is permanent. Most riders need it once.
How are trails recommended?Closed A different question, asked by the riders who want to know why a route came back the way it did. Not everyone asks.

The last line of the second twisty is the one that earns its keep. Panguitch's main connected component is 57% of nodes — 43% of the network is fragments joined to nothing. A rider who knows a trail is out there and never sees it used will assume the app is broken.

Saying it plainly turns a bug report into an understood limitation.

Peaks left out of the "reaches" list deliberately. Fred, 08-23: "a view is not something you route to get to." Only 10% of peaks sit within 200 ft of a trail, so listing them would promise something the router mostly cannot deliver.

Both states persist as preferences (ai_tw_how_open default true, ai_tw_rec_open default false) — not in the draft JSON, or they would travel in the GPX to another rider's phone.
