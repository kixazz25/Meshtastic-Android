2 · AI Design panel — NEW

NEW SCREEN Full-screen, opened from AI DESIGN w/ RIDER GUIDANCE. The route toolbar hides while it is open so the panel has the screen to itself.

It opens by saying what suggestions are based on — the trail data for the area, the natural features on it (springs, summits, cliffs, cones), and the points of interest.

Two ways to ask

OptionWhat it does
Explore the area You give a starting point and how long you want to be out. GroupTrack picks everything else — the rides that take in the most features for the distance.
Include places I choose You drop the places that have to be in the ride. GroupTrack draws routes containing as many of your points as it can within the distance limit, then backfills with as many other features as it can accommodate. ⚠ NOT BUILT YET.

What you set

  • A name for these routes. GroupTrack appends Route 1, Route 2 and so on. You rename whichever you keep.
  • How far you want to ride — a range. A wide range gives more choices; it is what the search has room to work with.
  • Your average speed — a range, default 12–18 mph.

⭐ THE SPEED WORDING MATTERS AND IS DELIBERATE. It is measured from real recorded rides across varied terrain, and it already includes the stops those riders took. So the estimate is the whole day out, not moving time. That is why the band is 12–18 and not 25–35 — the machines go faster, but the rides average out this way once stops are counted.

Riders are told: if your group gets back earlier or later than the estimate, change this number and the next suggestion fits better.

⚠ Narrowing the speed range does not change which rides come back — only how tightly the time is estimated. The manual should say so, or riders will tighten it expecting better routes.

Time is never entered. It is worked out from the mileage and speed, and shown as an envelope: "every ride suggested will be between X and Y hours."

The results, in the same panel

The panel does not close after searching — it switches to showing what came back: how many routes, their names, each with distance, riding time, feature count and mix. They are saved as work in progress; keep one and discard the rest.

CONTINUE returns to the route in progress.

⚠ "It has not seen the ground." The panel carries this and the manual must not soften it: GroupTrack knows a trail is there. It does not know how wide it is, what the surface is like, or what is on it — that is not in the trail data at all. Measured: surface is populated on about 30% of trails, width is not in the data, and motorized designation is empty in every source imported.

On an OHV route that is a safety line, not a disclaimer. It is deliberately phrased as what the app does not know rather than "ride at your own risk", because the first is useful and the second is noise riders learn to skip.

3 · Route DETAIL button and panel — NEW

NEW A round DETAIL button on the upper right, under Help. It only appears when the route you have open actually has a description — routes you draw yourself do not get one, so no button.

It opens a scrollable panel describing the ride:

  • A headline — distance, hours, and what is on it
  • Distance, riding time, feature count, how much doubles back
  • What you will see — the features in plain words (summit, spring, volcanic cone)
  • In order — each one with its mile, with features at the same spot grouped
  • Ground covered — which named trails, and how much is unnamed track
  • Before you go — unconfirmed junctions, and how much of the ride is on unnamed tracks

⭐ Internally this is one panel that will also serve saved routes and eventually trail notes — it takes prepared content rather than reading any particular source. Riders will not see that, but it means the same screen appears in more places later.

4 · What a suggested route looks like on the map

No new screen, but worth a manual note: a suggested route is saved as a work-in-progress route and appears in the route picker like any draft. Open it in the planner to see it, keep it or discard it, and rename it when you save.

INTERNAL Routes now carry a vertex at each junction plus one ten feet along the trail being joined, so the line follows turns correctly. Riders will not notice this except that routes no longer cut corners at junctions.

Release-notes wording, when it is ready

Suggested phrasing for the eventual notes — hold until the flow is finished:

Route building has two ways now. Draw it yourself by dropping points along the trails, as before — or let GroupTrack work out rides for you from the trail data, natural features and points of interest already on your map. Say roughly how far you want to ride and it will offer several rides to choose from, each saved as work in progress so nothing is committed until you keep one.

Each suggested ride comes with a description — what you will see, at what mile, which named trails it uses, and what to check before you go.

⚠ Keep the "it has not seen the ground" paragraph in the notes as well as the panel. It is the thing a rider most needs to know and the thing most easily lost in a summary.
