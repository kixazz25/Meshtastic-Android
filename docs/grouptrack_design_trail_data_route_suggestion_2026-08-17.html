⭐ WHY THIS EXISTS. The 2.7 "most interesting route" feature was moved up into 2.6g on the reasoning that we are already inside route handling and the authority/install work that pushed it out resolved in two days rather than the protracted slog budgeted for. 1. What the trail data actually contains

⛔ Finding 1 — the OSM import carries geometry and name, nothing else

Rows in trails from the OSM imports have carto_code and source_id both empty. No class, no grade, no width, no access. The extract's class list (track, track_grade1–5, path, bridleway, unclassified) was used to decide what to keep and then discarded rather than written to the row.

Scale of it: 112,696 trails with no carto_code against ~47,600 classified. The classified ones are the Utah government trail dataset from the V2.5 work, carrying that agency's permitted-use taxonomy. The OSM imports never had a mapping written, because the two vocabularies describe different things.

⛔ Finding 2 — OSM grade does NOT mean OHV permission. They are orthogonal.

tracktype (grade1–5) classifies surface firmness, construction and maintenance — grade1 hardest/paved, grade5 unimproved. It began life as a passability measure and drifted to firmness. Legal access is a different tag entirely (access, motor_vehicle, atv, ohv/orv, maxwidth), and access defaults are country- and land-manager-specific.

⚠ In arid regions specifically — i.e. Utah and Arizona — the OSM wiki notes mappers often cannot apply the drainage/maintenance criteria that separate grade3 from grade4 and fall back on firmness or smoothness. So the middle of the scale is least reliable exactly where we operate.

What OSM does give us: highway=track implies the way is wide enough for a typical four-wheeled vehicle. path and bridleway do not. That is a real rideability signal even with no permission data.

⚠ Finding 3 — the free Geofabrik extract cannot supply permission or width

gis_osm_roads_free columns, read from the file:

fid · geom · osm_id · code · fclass · name · ref · oneway · maxspeed · layer · bridge · tunnel

No motor_vehicle, no atv, no ohv, no maxwidth, no separate tracktype. Those tags exist in OSM but are not in the free export. Getting them means the full PBF or Geofabrik's paid extended export — a different pipeline, real work.

⭐ But fclass IS present, and Geofabrik folds tracktype into it: the values are exactly track_grade1–5, track, path, bridleway, unclassified. The grade data we thought was missing is in the file we already have. It is one field away from being usable.

2. The rideability rule (settled)

Fred's stance, and the data forces it anyway: "error on the side of inclusive" and "go one layer up in classification just to be more inclusive." Excluding a rideable trail is worse than including a marginal one — a rider can look at a route and reject it, but cannot see what was filtered out.

DecisionApplies to
INCLUDEtrack + all track_grade* + unclassified
EXCLUDEpath + bridleway (17,038 ways — not vehicle-width)
GRADEa weak SCORING term, never a filter, applied only where present

⭐ Grade-as-score rather than grade-as-filter also keeps the network connected: a single grade5 link between two grade2 stretches must not break a route. The suggester prefers firmer ground when it has a choice and accepts rough ground to complete a good loop.

THE CHANGE THIS REQUIRES: carry fclass into the trails table at import. One field, in a pipeline already built. It pays off three times — the path/bridleway exclusion, the map colouring something meaningful instead of 70% cyan, and the routing objective getting its rideability input.

⚠ Also worth pulling from the same GPKG while there: gis_osm_water_a_free (lakes/reservoirs as scenic attractors — we currently only have springs as points) and gis_osm_protected_areas_a_free. The second is the one permission signal the free extract does give us: designated wilderness prohibits motorized use entirely, so a route crossing it is a hard error, not a preference.

The objective function

Maximize interestingness subject to constraints — not minimize distance. That inversion is the differentiator; existing nav optimizes shortest/fastest and cannot easily do this because they lack OHV trail data and the objective is wrong for them.

Hard constraints (a candidate fails): total distance within 45–60 mi · estimated ride time 5–9 h (RideCalc, 10–15 mph) · passes through all assigned drop points · connected on the trail graph.

⚠⚠ THE CONCLUSION THAT MATTERS: v1 is carried by scenic proximity, loop closure, variety and the distance/time envelope. Grade is a tiebreaker on a third of the network. "Trail quality and difficulty" — named first when the feature was described — is not available and should not be claimed.

Open design decisions: (a) buffer distance for "passes near" — a peak two miles off trail is not part of the ride; half a mile is a starting value, needs tuning. (b) Does v1 produce ONE route or 3–4 distinct ones? Multi-route with coverage across all drop points is a substantially harder optimization than best-single-route. Recommendation: v1 = one route per drop-point set, and the user groups the points themselves.

⛔ The safety boundary — non-negotiable

For Arizona there is no permission data at all, and OSM lags real-world closures everywhere. Routing a rider onto a trail where motorized use is prohibited — by travel management plan, wilderness designation, or private land — is a different class of error from a dull route.

Any suggested route built from OSM-only trails must say so in the UI and point at the land manager's MVUM / BLM designation as the authority. This is not legal cover; it is the difference between a useful tool and one that strands someone on a closed trail.

4. The width ruler (Fred, 2026-08-17)

⭐ The problem it solves: at z16 and below on satellite imagery a rider can tell a hiking/biking/bridle trail from a vehicle track, but cannot tell a 54" trail from a 72" one — and that is exactly the fact OSM cannot supply either.

The mechanic: a rotatable on-map reference, positioned along a trail, sized in real-world units. Meters-per-pixel is known exactly at any zoom and latitude, so the scale is exact even though the imagery is not.

Two bands, not one and not a tick scale: inner mark at 50", outer at 72" — the UTV range. Three readable outcomes: narrower than inner · between the bands · wider than outer. Between-the-bands is the honest "go check the MVUM" signal rather than a number to trust.

⛔ Deliberately NOT a tick-marked scale. Ticks invite reading a measurement off imagery that cannot support one. The tool answers "which side of these two widths", not "how many inches".

⚠ Honest limits — these belong in the UI, not just this doc

  • Imagery resolution is the real limit, not zoom. Google Hybrid is roughly 0.3–0.6 m/px in rural terrain. 50" = 1.27 m ≈ 2–4 px; 72" = 1.83 m ≈ 3–6 px. Beyond a point you are looking at upsampled tiles, not more detail — the tool is honest at some scales and misleading at others, and should say which.
  • The gap between the bands is only ~0.56 m — a pixel or two. They will often look nearly coincident. That is the correct behaviour: it shows the rider the distinction is at the edge of what the imagery resolves. The visual must survive near-overlap without looking broken.
  • What you see is the disturbed corridor, not the legal tread — often wider than the tread, sometimes narrower under tree cover. Eight feet of visible scar may be a 50" trail with braiding.
  • Erring large is the right failure mode. Fred: "error on the large side, 74" vs 50" — that should be discernable." A marginal trail gets flagged for verification rather than a rider being sent home on a false negative, and a wide machine is never told a narrow trail fits.
  • Colour: desert imagery is tan/orange, so warm colours vanish. Cyan + magenta hold up on arid ground and stay distinguishable for most colour-vision types. Avoid a red/green pair.

Sequencing: the ruler is a 2D overlay computed from meters-per-pixel — it works on the existing Leaflet map today, standalone, with no dependency on the renderer decision. Build it in 2.6g. When MapLibre lands it gains terrain context for free.

5. Terrain — why it is 2.7, and what it drags with it

3D terrain would address what the data cannot: no elevation column, no grade on 68% of tracks, no width. A rider could judge steepness, exposure and drainage crossings directly — which is most of what "is this rideable" actually means.

But it rides on the MapLibre swap, the largest single piece on the roadmap, and the convoy tracking rewrite depends on the same decision. The ruler does not need to wait for it; terrain does.

⚠ For the 2.7 scoping, flag now: terrain rendering needs an elevation source (DEM tiles / terrain-RGB) — that is another offline data store alongside the 50–60 GB of imagery, so it is a storage and download-pipeline question as much as a rendering one. It belongs in the MapLibre scoping rather than being discovered afterwards.

5b. CORRECTIONS to sections 1–2 (same session, later in the conversation)

⛔ fclass as an EXCLUSION FILTER is WITHDRAWN

Section 2 above proposed excluding path and bridleway using fclass. Fred's objection kills it: "it would not add it as allowed usage, it would eliminate some trails from the planning process and we have no idea what some is."

Acting on a crowd-sourced classification with no accuracy guarantee means removing trails from planning based on a tag we cannot audit. That is a worse failure than the one it fixes. A hiking trail appearing in the candidate set is VISIBLE — the rider sees it and rejects it. A rideable trail silently filtered out is INVISIBLE. It also contradicts the inclusive stance the same section adopts.

If fclass has a role it is a display hint or a soft score — colour a trail differently, rank it lower, leave it visible and selectable. That is a much smaller claim and does not justify an upsert redesign plus a two-state re-import.

⚠ The re-import is NOT a prerequisite — sequence corrected

Section 7 lists carrying fclass as item 3, a gate before the suggester. That was wrong. Two reasons:

  • The exclusion it was meant to enable is withdrawn (above), so most of its value evaporates.
  • The Utah subset already has this signal. carto_code drives the map colours today (yellow Hiking Only, purple Biking Only). On the 30% with Utah data, exclusion information already exists. fclass would only add it for the OSM 70%.

And the mechanics are worse than assumed: SpatialDbManager.kt:1659 is INSERT OR IGNORE INTO trails (...) and the column list does not include carto_code at all. Against UNIQUE(geom_hash) a re-import would skip every existing row and change nothing. Carrying a new field needs an upsert or a targeted UPDATE keyed on geom_hash — bulk writes to a spatial DB that has been emptied three times this month, with refuse-to-create still unbuilt.

⭐ Net: the trail-data work comes OFF the critical path. It is a route-quality improvement, not a gate. ⭐ Extract-time filtering was raised as an alternative (no upsert, no bulk write — the extract already filters, candidate_rows 201757 → kept 104186). Right mechanism, wrong policy for a classification we do not trust: it makes the exclusion permanent AND invisible. Better version: carry fclass at extract and filter nothing — the field lands, everything stays visible, the app decides reversibly.

⚠ Context that softened this: trails visible on the basemap imagery are NOT limited to what is in the DB. A rider always sees more than we catalogue, and can evaluate anything with the width ruler. So DB exclusion removes a trail from the ROUTING CANDIDATE SET, not from view. ⬜ OPEN: does the planner allow drawing a route over terrain with no DB trail underneath, or does route-building SNAP to catalogued trails? If it snaps, an excluded trail really is unusable for planning and the ruler only helps evaluate something that cannot be planned through.

5c. BLM RESTRICTIONS — the real permission source (researched 2026-08-17)

⭐⭐⭐ Fred's framing, which is the correct one: what we want to process is BLM restrictions, by area or trail — and BLM only has authority over BLM land. This is a RESTRICTIONS layer, not a geometry source. BLM was previously evaluated and set aside on coverage grounds (OSM and UGRC give far more trails) — that judgement was about GEOMETRY. The designation attributes are a different axis and nothing else provides them.

What BLM publishes

All downloadable from the BLM Geospatial Business Platform Hub as Shapefile, File Geodatabase, GeoJSON, KML, CSV or GeoServices. For two states that is two polygon downloads, not fifty.

⭐⭐ "LIMITED" IS THE DESIGNATION THAT MATTERS, and it is almost certainly dominant in our riding areas. It does not mean vaguely-restricted — it means you must stay on designated routes. So an area being Limited is NOT permission to plan freely within it. This is precisely where the two layers compose:

  • Area polygons answer can I be here at all — Closed blocks outright, Open permits free routing, Limited means routes-only
  • GTLF routes resolve the Limited case — which specific routes carry the designation

This is the first thing that lets the planner say something AFFIRMATIVE rather than "not obviously prohibited."

⛔ Limits that belong in the UI, not just this doc

  • BLM land only. USFS MVUM is a separate dataset; state, tribal and private land are covered by neither. Outside BLM land the layer is SILENT, and silence must not read as "open." Shading only BLM-restricted areas means unshaded ground includes land under entirely different rules.
  • BLM explicitly says to verify current limitations with the local field office, as restrictions may vary from what the data depicts.
  • Routes may be additionally limited by time and/or season — so "Open" is not unconditional.
  • Some legacy field-office datasets carry scale limits (e.g. not to be used beyond 1:24,000).

The planner behaviour Fred specified

Highlight restricted areas in colour, and block vertex dropping inside them. Mechanically: polygon overlay for display, point-in-polygon on tap for the block.

  • Blocking silently is worse than blocking with a reason. A tap that does nothing reads as a bug. Show the area name and its designation — same reasoning as the authority gate's declined screen.
  • A route can CROSS a closed area with no vertex inside it. Two points either side and the path between crosses. So the polygon test must also run on the route line, not just on the tap.
  • Label honestly. Until MVUM is ingested, the layer shows BLM designations — say that, rather than "restricted areas", which implies coverage we do not have.

5d. THE SOURCE REGISTRY + BBOX SELECTION MODEL (Fred, 2026-08-17)

⭐⭐ The design: rather than a per-state checklist, the user selects an AREA; sources are chosen by intersecting that area against each source's recorded coverage boundary; each selected source is then imported within the same bbox. Ride area, not administrative geography — which matches how people plan, since trips cross state lines without anyone caring.

TWO ENTRY POINTS, ONE PIPELINE. States have bboxes, so "pick Utah" and "draw a rectangle" both resolve to a bbox. The state list is a convenience layer over well-known extents — no separate code path, no parallel checklist to maintain.

The registry already exists and is already the right shape. trail_sources: source_id · name · agency · url · format · trail_types · coverage_state · boundary · approved · last_checked_at. Someone designed exactly this and never populated it. Seeding it makes the "what sources cover this area" checklist a QUERY rather than a maintained document.

What this forces us to design

  • boundary must be populated per source before intersection is possible. Geofabrik extracts have state bounds, UGRC is Utah, GTLF is national, BLM area layers are per-state. Some are rectangles, some real polygons.
  • The registry needs a kind alongside format. Sources produce different things — trails, restriction polygons, route designations. bbox selection is uniform; what happens after it branches by kind. Restrictions are NOT trails and must not be forced into trails/trail_properties — they have their own lifecycle and update cadence, and forcing them in is how a fourth thing fails to fit later.
  • format is the real work. GPKG, Shapefile, GeoJSON and ArcGIS REST are four fetch-and-parse paths. Listing a source is data entry; importing it is not.
  • State bbox ≠ state. Utah's rectangle clips corners of NV/AZ/CO/WY. ⭐ Fred: "that's ok, better more than not enough — our trails already show completely if they cross state lines." So spillover is a feature. ⬜ Worth confirming clipped trails arrive WHOLE in the routing graph too, or the suggester would treat a clipped segment as a dead end.

Natural starting point, testable against data already held: populate trail_sources for the two sources in use, with boundaries, and confirm the intersection picks them correctly. No new source required to prove the model.

6. RideCalc and the ride envelope (settled 2026-08-17)

RideCalc is PURE EXTRACTION — no new capability. One calculation applied to any polyline, three callers: artifact detail (tracks and trails), Route+ (planned routes), and the suggester (candidate routes). Exactly the shape the overriding module rule wants, and the reason it must be extracted before the suggester rather than alongside it — it is currently duplicated across artifact detail and Route+, so building on the duplicate means doing it twice.

ParameterRoleAdjustable
Speed range (mph)Estimation only — a RANGE, not a point value✅ default in settings, user-adjustable
Distance bounds (mi)The hard constraint the suggester satisfies — upper AND lower✅ default in settings, user-adjustable
Ride timeDerived output, reported as a range— never entered directly

⭐ Why distance carries the constraint and time does not: the two are coupled through speed, so making both hard bounds creates a which-one-bound-first problem and a route that silently fails the limit the rider cared about. One constraint to satisfy, one range to report. A 55-mile suggestion reports e.g. 4.5–6.5 h rather than a single figure that will be wrong.

⚠ The LOWER bound matters as much as the upper and is easy to overlook: without it the suggester satisfies "under 60 miles" with a 12-mile loop that technically touches every drop point. The lower bound is what makes it a day's ride rather than a shortcut.

Why both are adjustable: an experienced group on fast desert two-track and a mixed group with newcomers on technical ground need different envelopes, and the app should not try to infer which. A rider who sets 6–10 mph gets an estimate reflecting their reality instead of a default that flatters it. Multi-day trip planning falls out of the same control rather than needing separate logic.

⬜ OPEN, not a blocker: the default speed range could be MEASURED from the 88 recorded tracks (real distances and durations, sitting in the 08-16 backup) rather than assumed at 10–15 mph. Cheap to compute, and it would make the estimate defensible instead of guessed — potentially per-rider, since someone's own history predicts their pace better than an average.

6. What this changes about the plan

  1. Route dedup comes OFF the 2.6g list — hash-naming is the dedup method, same as tracks. Already solved by the naming scheme.
  2. The second route-mode disarm path comes OFF as a separate item — it is absorbed by the discard/save consolidation. Standardize the five, leave the exit/return decision in the calling stream. ⭐ Consequence: "did we find every disarm path" stops being a discoverable question and becomes a classification question — every one of the six sites gets a deliberate exit-or-stay answer. Nothing left to inference. That is the actual fix for the tangle.
  3. Carry fclass at import — new, small, and it unblocks rideability, the map colouring, and the routing objective at once.
  4. RideCalc extraction moves ahead of the suggester — the suggester needs the time envelope, and RideCalc is currently duplicated across artifact detail and Route+. Build on the duplicate and you either do it twice or block later.
  5. The width ruler is new scope, standalone, 2D, no renderer dependency.
  6. The suggester's claim shrinks to what the data supports: scenic + loop + variety + envelope. Still a real differentiator — big nav optimizes shortest/fastest and lacks OHV trail data — but "trail quality and difficulty" is not part of v1.
