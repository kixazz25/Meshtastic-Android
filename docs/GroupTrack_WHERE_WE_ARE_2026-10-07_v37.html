The direction, in one paragraph

2.7 = separate GroupTrack from the mesh: level 1 (standalone GroupTrack) stands alone; the mesh is a plugin (level 2). Inside it, 2.7 delivers TAK packets and the first IP-configured mesh radios (the Nucleus); configuration over Bluetooth, USB and IP by one update process.

GroupTrack is separating from its Meshtastic origins. Release 2.7 makes the mesh one transport among two — LoRa radios and the Nucleus (Natak's IP-mesh box) — and moves rider identity and position into TAK-format messages sent by the app. A ride is one JSON file made on the leader's tablet and applied to every device from that file. The standalone T1000-E path is kept working on the new channel but is being sunset; the aim is every rider on an app-attached radio.

09-25, sharpened: GroupTrack receives one common payload — a TAK position report — from any source. Radios configured by GroupTrack (TAK_TRACKER, now the default) speak TAK by themselves, with the rider's callsign in every report; the only Meshtastic-specific piece left is converting raw GPS broadcasts, behind a legacy switch in the plugin. "Me" is resolved first on every tick. ⚖ Direction for the licence boundary: option A — the mesh side as a separate plugin app talking TAK. ⚠ This changes the T1000-E premise: a naked T1000 now speaks TAK — the sunset may apply only to the old tool.

The system at 30,000 ft

  • Two worlds, one bridge: the LoRa mesh on the ride's channel, and the Nucleus Wi-Fi (SSID = the network id), joined by the Nucleus bridge.
  • Every device transmits once, in one message type: app-attached radios send TAK only; T1000-Es send GPS position only CORRECTED 09-25: every radio GroupTrack configures sends TAK itself (TAK_TRACKER, callsign included) — app-attached or not; the app sending TAK is optional. Raw GPS (radios GroupTrack did not configure) is converted to TAK by the plugin, behind a legacy switch.
  • Two networks with two jobs: GroupTrack — the default, for setup and control, a unit's home; Group+6 — one per ride, its own 256-bit key.
  • Apply writes only what we manage, records everything, and reports other differences as a document. Every apply starts from the ride JSON.
  • One map entry per device, keyed by the radio's node number (or a TAK-uid key for Wi-Fi-only riders); labelled by callsign. 09-25: a device not heard within the Settings slider's hours never becomes an entry — ONE staleness rule, decided at the read. Names only from reports (designed: the rider table, in the plugin).
  • The preset decides group size: LONG_FAST ≈ 1–2 riders at 3 s; MEDIUM_FAST ≈ 5–6; SHORT_FAST ≈ 13+ — at the cost of range. One preset per ride. 09-25 measured: two radios at 3 s on LONG_FAST ≈ 20% channel utilisation; the default reports every 15 s standing / 3 s moving (20 m) — 8–10 s standing measured indoors.
  • The rider profile is foundational — the first screen; the callsign is the map label, the radio's name and the TAK identity.

10-07 — the AI route problem was the map key, and every release now passes our own tablets first

The tester's problem, solved: one tester kept landing back at the start of AI route design and never saw the compare screen. After a long day of fixes in the wrong place, the cause turned out to be simple: he had switched every trail type off in the map key and placed his trailhead on one of his own tracks. GroupTrack builds AI routes only on the trails the map shows — so it had nothing to work with, found no routes, and quietly started over. Nothing was broken on his tablet.

The fix going in next (release f): when a rider picks a trailhead for AI design, GroupTrack checks for a trail shown on the map within half a mile. If there isn't one, it says so right there — turn trail types on, or pick a trailhead closer to a trail — instead of searching and silently failing. The day's extra "clear" buttons are being removed again: once the real cause was known, they weren't needed.

What testers have now (release e): the radio fix from last night (a parked radio no longer drops and reconnects), TOPO+ on OpenTopoMap, sturdier compare sets, and AI design that clears its own leftovers before it starts.

How we release from now on: everything is built and released from the Mac, uploaded to Play from the Mac — and nothing goes to testers until it has been installed and tried on one of our own tablets. Today proved why.

Radios: the overnight test confirmed the reconnect fix; both test radios now run the planned settings (relays limited to two hops, a report every 30 seconds when parked). The public release (Thursday or Friday) waits on one clean overnight test with these settings.

10-06 — GroupTrack is complete without a radio; group riding is the add-on

The principle: most riders (about 9 in 10) will never use a mesh radio, and they get the whole of GroupTrack: plan routes, create and share rides, download maps, navigate and record. Group riding — radios, check-in, seeing everyone on the map — is an add-on you switch on when you have a radio: "Do you have a mesh radio?" at install, and a Settings box "Activate GRP Awareness plugin". Learn the app first; the group is one more thing it can do, not a tangle of screens you don't need yet.

The road, corrected:

  • 2.7a (finishing now): rides, ride sharing, and the radio fixes from this week — plus one more for radio stability: the app tunes how often each radio reports to the size of the group, so up to 10 radios share the air without crowding it.
  • 2.7b — the separation: everything that talks to radios moves into its own part of the app, behind that on/off switch. Riders without a radio never meet it.
  • 2.8: the radio side gets its new engine (the standard TAK message format, no constant polling, a clean boundary) and the groundwork for Apple.
  • 2.9: GroupTrack on iPad (the full app) and Mac (the planning station).

10-04 — 2.7a gets sturdy: the radio can fail, the ride carries on

What happened: on 10-03 our testers had a bad day on the older software. Their radios went quiet while they sat still, the app kept believing the radio was connected, and riders ended up with no position on the screen. That is the panic we never want a rider to feel.

What we built in a day and a half, and what riders now get:

  • The ride carries on without the radio. If the radio stops answering, GroupTrack quietly switches to the tablet's own GPS. Your cart keeps moving under your name, the maps stay, and the rest of the group stays on screen where they were last seen.
  • A red light tells the truth. GRP Awareness turns red whenever the radio isn't really talking. GroupTrack tries to reconnect on its own, and only if that fails asks the rider for one simple step.
  • The ride map never opens blank. It always finds something to show: your cart, your last area, or your first GPS fix.
  • Radios that wouldn't connect now do. A hidden fault left from renaming the app in July stopped radios from ever finishing a connection. Only closing the app helped. It's fixed.
  • Every radio keeps its real name. GroupTrack now remembers each radio's current name, so riders see "fred&June" or "Billy beans", not "Meshtastic_deda", even after a disconnect.
  • Simpler pairing. Radios now pair with no PIN, so a radio and a tablet can't quietly fall out of step. Each radio needs one re-pair the first time.

Where it stands: built, proven on our two test tablets, and in our internal testers' hands. Next: the planning map can still open white for someone who goes straight to it offline before the GPS wakes up. That's first tomorrow, then tester feedback, then the public launch.

What we learned: these radios can look healthy while being quietly out of step, and a rider can't tell. So GroupTrack has to notice for them, say so plainly, and keep the ride going. That's now the rule.

10-03 — how we proceed: 2.8

The next big step is GroupTrack 2.8 — a major revision built on the Mac, while the released 2.7 stays on the PC for fixes. 2.8 separates GroupTrack from the radio behind a small, open set of services, so the same GroupTrack can run with a mesh radio, over the internet, or on an iPad.

Riders will notice three things: a Group riding switch in Settings (set by the install question, changeable any time); a group map that updates the moment a report arrives instead of every few seconds; and GroupTrack speaking CoT, the language of the TAK world — so other TAK apps can see a GroupTrack rider, and GroupTrack can see them.

First comes design: every service defined with what goes in and what comes out, mapped to the Android code it replaces and to the Apple components that will provide it on the iPad — proven before any of it is built.

10-02 night — the road beyond the launch

The next release (2.7b) begins with a separation: GroupTrack and the radio become two parts that never touch directly, joined by a small set of services — connect, configure, send and receive the group's messages. Android keeps its proven radio code underneath; riders see no change. It must work over the mesh radio and over the internet before anything else is built on it.

Then: a single-rider GroupTrack (planning and riding, with a Nucleus), and after that an iPad GroupTrack with a Group Comm plugin — our open-source radio component — so iPad riders join the same group on the same map. First, on the Mac, a short series of experiments proves the iPad can configure a radio exactly as Android does.

The principle: everything that passes between devices and services is an open, published format; the work done on it stays inside GroupTrack. That opens doors: a GroupTrack ride — route, trailhead, waypoints and the exact maps to download — can be opened by other apps such as onX and Gaia, and GroupTrack's route planning could become a service for partners, clubs and outfitters. GroupTrack shares rides, not trail data, and credits OpenStreetMap.

How the doc set works

Living documents are EDITED IN PLACE and re-versioned — the new version contains everything the old one had, plus the day's work. So the highest version number is always the complete one, and older copies can be ignored.

The handoff is the exception: it is rewritten each time, which is why it must carry every open item forward. v1 today did not; v2 does.

Filenames are never reused — a correction gets a new version number, because Windows appends "(1)" to a duplicate download and the wrong file gets run.

Today's design and every decision reaches the LIVING MASTER, not just a side document — a living doc that only references an external file is no use if that file is lost.
