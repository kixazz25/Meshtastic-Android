Candidate routes — shortlist, compare, promote

Design, 2026-08-26 · CANDIDATES-2026-08-26 · supersedes the four-route results list

⛔ DEPENDS ON THE ORIGIN FIX. None of this is worth building while every route starts from one arbitrary spoke — nine candidates that all leave in the same direction is a worse experience than four, not a better one. Fix the origin first, then build this.

1. Where the candidates come from

Every trail within 200 yards of the trailhead is a candidate start, not just the one nearest node. Each start gets its own search, so each spoke produces its own route sequences.

You may get four routes east and three north — and that asymmetry is information, not a fault. It says where the riding is.

⭐ THIS REPLACES THE PENALTY MECHANISM. Today route 2 is found by penalising route 1's edges (PENALTY = 2.2), which pushes the search away from the hub rather than down a different spoke. Independent searches from each start make the routes different by construction rather than by discouragement.

⚠ Open: 200 yards from the pin, or 200 yards along each trail? A trail passing 50 ft away and one passing 190 yards away are both candidates, but the second starts the ride 190 yards from where the rider stood. And each start is a full 2,500-restart search — four starts is 10,000. Measure the cost before fixing the candidate count.

⭐ THE SEARCH MAY PRODUCE TWENTY. THE PANEL SHOWS THE NINE HIGHEST. Fred, 08-26. Four starts producing five sequences each is twenty candidates — that is the normal case, not an edge case. Every candidate is scored, ranked across all starts, and the top nine surface.

⚠ Ranking across starts, not within them. If the east spoke genuinely holds the six best rides, the rider should see six east routes — not one per direction for fairness. The asymmetry is the information.

⭐ AND THE RIDER ALREADY HAS THE CONTROL FOR THIS: THE PINS. Fred, 08-26: "if the rider wants focus he drops pins to control the area." Balancing directions artificially would be the app second-guessing a rider who has not asked for anything. A rider who wants the ride to go north drops a pin north — and the high-value user POI pulls the search there by scoring, which is the mechanism that already exists.

So: no direction quota, no spreading, no fairness rule. Unpinned means "show me the best rides here"; pinned means "these places must be in it". Two clear modes, and the rider chooses which by dropping pins or not.

⭐ HOW TO SAY IT TO A RIDER: PINS PROVIDE FOCUS TO THE SEARCH. Fred, 08-26. Same functionality, and it is what a rider can actually hold in their head — "Drop pins on the areas or features you want included."

That wording is doing real work: "areas or features" covers both ways a rider thinks about it. Sometimes they know the exact thing — a spring, an overlook, a climb. Sometimes they only know the part of the map they want the ride to spend time in. Both are legitimate and the app treats them the same way.

The mechanism underneath is unchanged and is two jobs at once: the pins bound the region worth searching, and each is a high-value POI the route is pulled to reach. But neither of those is a sentence a rider needs. Focus is.

⚠ The boundary is DERIVED FROM the pins, never a filter that excludes them — and it must carry mileage headroom. Ten pins inside a 10-mile circle with a 55–80 mile band means the ride has to range well beyond the pins' own extent. Boundary from pins PLUS headroom, not pins alone. Tightening the region to the pins is what starves the search.

⚠ Open: are the other eleven written as WIPs too, or discarded at ranking? Writing twenty and showing nine means eleven files nobody asked for; discarding means a route that ranked tenth is gone for good. Leaning: rank first, write only the nine.

1b. ⭐⭐ THE ALGORITHM — enumerate, filter, score (Fred, 08-26)

This replaces the randomised greedy entirely. No gain-per-mile ordering, no beam pick, no 2,500 restarts, no route-level PENALTY.

  1. List the POIs in range.
  2. List the starting points — every trail within 200 yards of the trailhead.
  3. Start with ALL POIs together, then work down through combinations: all, then every set of N−1, then N−2, and so on.
  4. One path per POI combination, per origin. Not 2–3 routings through the same POIs — the variation comes from the origins, which is the point of multi-start.
  5. Throw out anything over the ceiling or under the floor.
  6. Score everything that survives, rank across all origins, surface the top nine.

⭐ WHY DESCENDING FROM ALL POIs. If the full set fits the mileage, it is the answer by construction — most features collected. Dropping a level only becomes necessary when the full set is too long, and the search stops at the first level that yields feasible routes rather than enumerating everything.

⚠ THE COMBINATORIAL COST IS THE THING TO MEASURE FIRST. With 18 POIs: 1 set at level 18, 18 at level 17, 153 at 16, 816 at 15. If the mileage only permits 8, that is 43,758 sets — each needing a tour solve, multiplied by the number of origins.

Fine at 12 POIs, not at 30. Measure before building: how many POIs are typically in range, and how deep the descent goes before routes fit.

⭐ Clustering is not a fault. The high scores may all be in one direction because that is where the riding is. The rider drops pins if they want focus — no direction quota, no spreading.

1c. ⭐⭐ THE SEARCH IS WATCHED ON THE MAP, NOT NARRATED (Fred, 08-26)

⭐ FIVE MINUTES IS FINE IF THE RIDER KNOWS WHAT IS HAPPENING. Fred, 08-26: "laying out 5 tracks in 5 minutes is ok if we post completion one track at a time ... the longer it takes the more messaging we must produce."

Silence is the problem, not duration. So the work is SHOWN on the map rather than described in a progress bar — and each step is real work completing, so progress is literal rather than guessed.

The sequence a rider watches

  1. Banner names the step — before each stage, so the rider knows what they are about to see happen.
  2. Every feature drops as a coloured pin — all of them in the corridor, coloured by type.
  3. Mileage elimination. Pins that cannot be reached out-and-back inside the ceiling vanish. Measured: 24 down to 13. The rider is seeing the true reach of an 80-mile ride.
  4. Combination elimination. Pins in no valid combination vanish — the bound prune, made visible.
  5. Survivors stay lit. These are what every route will be built from.
  6. Routes draw one at a time, each in its own colour, appearing as they are found rather than all at the end.

⭐ STEP 4 IS THE ONE THAT TEACHES SOMETHING. A pin that survives mileage and then dies on combinations is telling the rider "you can reach this, but not together with anything else worth having." That is real terrain knowledge and it is completely invisible today.

⭐ And the rider keeps what they learn. By the time six routes are drawn they understand where the features are, which trails they could have started from, and what an 80-mile ride actually reaches from that staging area.

⚠ PINS VANISH, THEY DO NOT GREY OUT (Fred, 08-26). Cleaner, and it keeps the map readable — 99 pins greyed is still 99 pins.

⚠ The banner comes BEFORE each step, not during. The rider is told what is about to happen, then watches it happen. A banner that appears afterwards is a report; one that appears first is an explanation.

⚠ WHERE THE TIME ACTUALLY GOES — measured 08-26 at 37.7235,-112.613. Sizes 13 down to 6 produced nothing and consumed most of the 67.8 seconds; all six rides appeared at size 5 in quick succession.

So the rider would watch a long "weighing combinations" phase and then six routes arrive almost together. That phase needs an honest count — combinations tested, ticking down — rather than a generic "working".

⭐ IT MERGES WITH THE COMPARISON PANEL. The routes are already drawn in distinct colours by the time the search ends, so selection and comparison happen on the same screen the search happened on. No transition, no separate results view.

⚠ Real work, though: the map must draw progressively from a background search, which is a different pattern from the current build-then-render.

2. The nine shown are saved as WIPs immediately

Nothing is lost to a crash or a back button. The WIPs are the safety net, not a decision.

⭐ SAVING STOPS BEING A DECISION. Today a rider must save a route before it can be seen on the map with the others — so every save is ambiguous: did they want this route, or did they just want to look at it? Writing all nine automatically makes the rider's selection the only decision that carries meaning.

⚠ THE MAP CANNOT TELL THEM APART, and that is the central problem. Fred, 08-26: "most or all cover the same ground." Nine lines over the same trails is spaghetti. What distinguishes them is what is ON them — which is why the narrative content belongs in the table, not behind a button.

Where they differ least, the differences matter most. If eight routes all read "2 summits and a spring", the one with three summits is the answer — and that must be visible without opening nine panels.

4. Compare — two or three, properly

Nine is a shortlist. The compare takes two or three finalists.

  1. Tick two or three in the panel
  2. Each gets a distinct colour on the map; the others hide
  3. The colour is echoed in the comparison, so a column and a line are visibly the same route
  4. Rule one out and hide it — reversible, it can be turned back on

⭐ THE COMPARISON IS AUTOMATED, AND IT SPLITS SHARED FROM UNIQUE.

Shared — features and trails all the selected routes hit. Over the same ground this is most of the content, and it is noise for the purpose of choosing.

Unique — what only one route reaches. This is the whole answer.

Three narratives printed side by side is not a comparison. Computing the difference is.

⭐ Colour is why the shortlist matters: three colours that read on satellite imagery is achievable; nine is not. Note that red is already taken — the Distance HUD and the rider's pins both use #FF0000.

4b. ⭐⭐ THE PANEL IS A LEGEND (Fred, 2026-08-26)

That single word settles the whole design. A legend is not a results list — it is the key that ties what is drawn on the map to what it is called.

So each row is: the route name, in the same colour as its line on the map. Nothing else is needed to know which is which.

What a row does

ElementBehaviour
Name, in the route's colourThe tie between the legend and the map. ⚠ Colour alone cannot be spoken about or listed; a name alone cannot be found on a map. Both, or the comparison does not work.
Tap the nameOpens that route's narrative — what is on it, at what mile, which named trails, how much doubles back.
Show / hideDraws or removes that route from the map. Reversible at any time.
SelectMarks it to be saved as a permanent route. ⚠ Multi-select — a rider who finds two good rides keeps both.

⭐ HIDE AND UNHIDE IS THE COMPARISON TOOL. Fred, 08-26: "look at two, look at three." Six routes drawn at once over the same ground is spaghetti — the rider narrows it by turning routes off, compares two or three, brings others back.

That is why hiding must be free and reversible. It is not a decision, it is how looking works. Nothing is discarded until the rider chooses at the end.

⚠ SIX COLOURS THAT READ ON SATELLITE IMAGERY. Harder than it sounds: #FF0000 is already spoken for by the Distance HUD and the rider's pins. The imagery underneath is green, tan and grey, and a route line is thin.

⭐ Hiding helps here too — with two or three shown, the colours only have to be distinct from each other and from the ground, not from five others as well.

4d. ⭐⭐ ONE BATCH AT A TIME — the open-batch lock

Fred, 2026-08-26: "suggest a json to not allow another route be created until this batch is completed and resolved — one set of six at a time."

Right, and it prevents a real mess. Six unresolved routes plus six more is twelve drafts with no way to tell which search produced which. The rider loses track and the WIP list becomes unusable by Friday.

The rules

RuleWhy
An open batch STOPS the route build and opens the batch on the map ⭐ Fred, 08-26: the JSON does not merely refuse — it launches the WIP map for that batch. The rider is taken to the six routes they already have, drawn and legended, and resolves them there. No dialog, no "you cannot do that": the app goes to the thing that is in the way.
No further route activity of ANY kind until it is resolved Not just the AI planner. Drawing a route by hand, starting another search, promoting from elsewhere — all of it waits. ⚠ One unresolved set at a time, by hand or automatically.
Per route: offered → saved or discarded The batch resolves when no route is still offered, and the file is then deleted. ⚠ Multi-select — several may be saved.
Resolved → the file is DELETED⭐ Fred, 08-26: no history. The saved routes are the record and they carry their own feature lists. Keeping resolved batches would accumulate files nobody opens and hold the rider's pins in two places.
⚠ Deliberately unlike the import manifests, which are kept because an import can fail silently and be found days later. A batch cannot — the rider is standing in front of it when it resolves.
The batch carries the REQUEST, not just the resultsStaging point, band, speed, pins, and where each pin was moved to. ⚠ Without this a rider cannot tell why a route came back the way it did, which is exactly what cost 08-25.

⚠ THE LOCK MUST BE ESCAPABLE. A batch left open by a crash would block the planner forever. Reopening must always offer "discard this batch and start again", and the file must be safe to delete by hand.

⚠ And it must not repeat the MARKERFIX lesson. The trails-clear marker was written into a folder that is emptied at startup, so it vanished and the clear ran on every launch. This file lives with the drafts it describes, not in a swept directory.

⭐ THE LOCK IS A REDIRECT, NOT A REFUSAL. That is the whole difference. A refusal leaves the rider holding a problem they did not know they had; a redirect puts them in front of it with the tools to clear it.

So the sequence is: rider asks for routes → an open batch exists → the WIP map opens on that batch with its six routes drawn, named and colour coded → they keep what they want and the rest go → the batch resolves → the planner is available again.

⚠ Resolution can be automatic — saving one route and dismissing the panel could discard the rest, if that is what the rider means. But it must be explicit and reversible until confirmed: six routes deleted by an accidental back-press is the same class of loss as the autosave-discard bug.

⭐ It also gives the WIP list something it lacks: a grouping. Today six drafts are six unrelated entries. With a batch id they are one search, with the request that produced them attached — which is what makes "show me the six" possible at all.

5. Promote one, delete the rest

  1. Rider picks the winner from the comparison
  2. It is promoted to a completed route
  3. The other eight are deleted — the WIP list does not accumulate nine per search

⚠ Open: does promotion delete the rest automatically, or offer it? Automatic keeps the list clean; offering protects a rider who wanted two. Leaning automatic with the deletion named on the button, so it is not a surprise.

⭐ Consistent with the pin decision (08-25): the rider's pins live in the WIP JSON and are dropped on promotion. Same principle — the WIP carries working state, the completed route carries only the ride.

6. What this needs that does not exist

  • Multiple routes drawn at once, in assigned colours. The map draws one route in progress today.
  • A shared-vs-unique diff over feature sets and named trails. The inputs exist — featureMix, featureCount, the trail list in RouteDetail — but nothing computes a difference across routes.
  • Direction as a route property. Not recorded today; it falls out of which start the search used, so it is free once candidate starts exist.
  • A candidate table that survives leaving the panel and coming back.

7. Sequence

  1. Origin fix — every trail within 200 yards is a candidate start. Nothing here works without it.
  2. Measure the cost of N searches, and fix the candidate count from that.
  3. Candidate table + rank-then-write-nine, replacing the four-route results list.
  4. Toggle-on-map with per-candidate colour.
  5. The automated compare — shared vs unique.
  6. Promote + delete the rest.
