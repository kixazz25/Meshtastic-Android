What this section covers. Everything that happens between installing GroupTrack and having trails on your map — and how to add more trails later, for a trip to another state or a specific riding area.

Your first launch

The first time you open GroupTrack it asks for two things, then offers to load trail data for your state. Once that finishes you land on the Ride Map with trails, scenic points and places already drawn.

  1. Storage and location access. GroupTrack keeps 50–60 GB of offline maps in shared storage so they survive updates and reinstalls, which is why it needs all-files access. Location is what puts you on the map and records your tracks.
  2. Home State trail data. GroupTrack works out which state you are in and offers to load its trails. One tap starts it.
  3. The Ride Map. Your trails are there when it finishes.

If you have used GroupTrack before and are reinstalling, your trails are still on the device — they live outside the app and an uninstall does not remove them. GroupTrack finds them and takes you straight to the map. You will not be asked to load anything.

Home State setup

GroupTrack uses your device's location to work out your state and shows you what it found:

Two choices:

  • LOAD [STATE] TRAIL DATA — start straight away for the state shown.
  • Select a different Home state — open the full list and pick another.

You need an internet connection for this. GroupTrack downloads trail data from several public sources, and one of them is a large file — typically 100–600 MB depending on the state. Use Wi-Fi if you can.

While it runs

GroupTrack processes every source that has trails in your state, one after another. The screen shows which source it is on, what it is doing, and how long it has been running.

MarkMeaning
▶working on this source now
○waiting its turn
✔finished

Leave GroupTrack open until it finishes. A banner at the top says the same thing. You do not need to do anything else — there is nothing to tap and nothing to watch for.

The time estimate is rough and usually pessimistic. A small state can finish in a few minutes; a large one with several sources can take half an hour.

When it finishes

Each source is listed with the number of records it added, and a total underneath. Tap OPEN RIDE MAP and your trails are there.

“0 · 43,348 already had” — what that means. When a source finds trails that are already on your device it does not add them twice. The first number is what was added; the second is what was already there. A row reading 0 · 1,004 already had is a source that worked perfectly and found nothing new — which is exactly what you want when you import the same ground twice.

A row reading 0 with no second number means that source has no trails in your state. Not every source covers every state.

The detail behind each source

Tap a source name to open its detail:

LineWhat it counts
Records processedEverything the source gave us
Records selectedHow many of those fell inside your state or area
DuplicatesAlready on your device — not added again
AddsNew records
Unprocessed errorsRecords that could not be read

Selected always equals duplicates plus adds plus errors. If you ever need to report a problem, this is the screen to describe.

If the import is interrupted

If GroupTrack closes part-way through — battery, a crash, a restart — nothing is damaged. Whatever was imported stays. Run the import again and GroupTrack will skip everything it already has.

If you skip it

You can back out of the setup. GroupTrack opens with an empty map and offers the setup again next time you start it. You can also import at any point from the Ride Planning Map — see below.

Importing trails later

On the Ride Planning Map, open Map Features and use IMPORT TRAILS BY STATE OR AREA.

The two buttons do different things.

  • IMPORT TRAILS BY STATE OR AREA — brings in trail data from public sources: agency trail systems and Open Source Maps.
  • IMPORT TRACKS, WAYPOINTS & ROUTES — brings in your own files: GPX exports from onX, Gaia or another app.

By State

Use this when you are going somewhere new — a trip to Colorado, a week in Utah. Search the list, pick the state, tap LOAD TRAIL DATA. It runs exactly like the first-time setup.

By Area

Use this when you want one riding area rather than a whole state — and when your ground crosses a state line.

GroupTrack opens the map with the drawing tools ready. Draw a box around the ground you want:

GroupTrack then works out which states your box reaches and processes every source that covers it — including a separate Open Source Maps download for each state.

A state may be listed without having trails in your area. States are matched using a rectangle drawn around each state, and those rectangles overlap. A neighbouring state can be included and then contribute nothing — it costs download time and nothing else. Your trails are unaffected.

Crossing a state line does not duplicate anything. A trail that runs from one state into the next appears in both states' data. GroupTrack recognises it as the same trail and keeps one copy.

Where the data comes from

GroupTrack combines several public sources. Which ones apply depends on where you are:

SourceWhat it covers
US Forest Service TrailsNational forest trail systems
US Geological Survey National TrailsThe USGS trail database
National Park Service TrailsNational park trail systems
Bureau of Land Management TrailsBLM routes and designations
State agency trailsVaries by state — Utah Trails and Pathways, Arizona State Parks & Trails, and others
Open Source MapsOpenStreetMap trails, unpaved roads, scenic points and places. Usually the largest source by a wide margin

There is no source checklist and nothing to choose. Every source that covers your ground runs, because that is what lets one source fill in what another is missing.
