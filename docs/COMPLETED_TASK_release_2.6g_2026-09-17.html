📋 2.7 — OWNERSHIP, POLYGONS AND ROUTE SCORING. POLYROUTE-2026-09-17

⚠ RELEASE ASSIGNMENT: Fred said "the follow-up 2.6g release" earlier in the session and "2.7" at the end. Recorded here under 2.7; confirm before planning.

THREE PIECES LAND TOGETHER:

  1. THE POLYGON DISPLAY LAYER. Private areas in red with ghosted diagonal stripes, behind a checkbox in the key. ⭐ More honest than reclassifying trails — it shows a rider WHY, and lets them see a boundary they are APPROACHING rather than discovering a trail simply is not offered. ⚠ Leaflet has no native hatch; it is an SVG pattern, needed on the frozen convoy map and the planner. ⚠ Polygons are light in COUNT, not always in VERTICES — simplification at import is what makes it work.
  2. THE OHV RESTRICTION LAYER — SEPARATE MEANING, SEPARATE CHECKBOX. BLM OHV_DSG_PUB_POLY and the USFS data with SEASONS OF USE. ⛔ One hatch cannot serve both: public land with OHV closed says go when the rider must not; private land with permission says stop when the trail is rideable.
  3. OWNERSHIP BEYOND UTAH — the western 10–12 states, because that is where the land pattern makes clutter removal necessary. ⭐ Reached twice independently: the trail-volume-vs-layer test, and the land-pattern argument.

⭐⭐⭐ AND THE ROUTING ANSWER, WHICH IS THE ELEGANT PART (Fred 09-17).

A trail crossing a restriction polygon takes −100 in the route scorer.

  • ⭐ Negative totals cannot be ridden. The sign carries the meaning — a scorer returning a negative number has said something CATEGORICAL, not just "worse".
  • ⭐ Bottom of the list, never presented. Same as the existing behaviour: only positives display. So nothing new is needed in the presentation layer at all.
  • ⭐⭐ IT IS NOT A NEW AVOIDANCE SYSTEM — it is another weight in the scorer that already exists. The 2.7 work is really just feed the polygons into the existing scorer.
  • ⭐ And it degrades gracefully with bad data: a wrong polygon costs a lower-ranked route, not a disappeared trail. Hard avoidance would silently remove legitimate ground with nothing to explain why.

⚠ ONE THING TO SETTLE AT BUILD TIME: whether a high-scoring route can ABSORB a single −100 and still come out positive. If a long route with many points of interest scores +150, one crossing leaves it at +50 and rideable — which would be wrong. Either the penalty scales with crossings, or −100 is applied as a FLOOR rather than a summand.

⚠ And if fewer than six routes score positive, present fewer — a short list is honest; backfilling from the negatives would put unrideable routes in front of a rider who assumes everything offered is valid.

⭐⭐ THE DEFAULT INVERTS: ALL TRAILS ARE PUBLIC UNLESS A SOURCE SAYS OTHERWISE. The release's job is to identify the PRIVATE ones.

⛔ This supersedes the 09-09 piece-5 framing ("only Utah has the ownership polygon layer, so land_status is null everywhere else and the private filter cannot work"). ⭐ THE TAG SOURCE IS ALREADY BUILT AND ALREADY MEASURED. access=private is the single best-populated tag in the extract — better covered than surface, tracktype or anything else, and well covered where Fred rides. 34,511 Utah ways carry it. ⛔⛔ DO NOT REVERSE THIS FROM THE 08-30 FINDING. READ THE DIRECTION.

08-30 measured that seeding MOTORIZED from tags failed decisively — 9,019 tag-confirmed motorized ways statewide and ZERO in Broken Ridge 2 and 3, the ground Fred actually rides. The conclusion recorded was "OSM access tagging is too sparse in western Utah to seed anything from."

⭐ That was the POSITIVE direction — prove a trail IS something. The default-public rule only needs the NEGATIVE direction — a trail is public unless a tag says shut — and access=private is the well-populated tag, unlike ohv/atv/motor_vehicle. Sparse tagging breaks inference, not exclusion. Without this distinction written down, someone reads "the access tags are too sparse" and reverses a correct design.

The sweep

ConditionAction
Every source section carries a countLeave process_state as written → move to history

⭐ ORDER IS LOAD-BEARING: stamp first, then move. Same reason OsmImportLedger.archiveLedger() renames before discardState() sweeps. Stamp fails → nothing moved, retried next launch. Move fails after a good stamp → a marked file sits in imports/, visible and recoverable. The state to avoid is a file in history nobody stamped, because then killed must be inferred rather than read.

⚠ "Has a count" must tolerate pre-08-21 manifests. The 08-20 NH manifest carries imported per source but none of the five recap counters and no manifest_id. Absent fields mean OLD, not broken. If the test requires the new fields, a device holding 43,348 records reads as never-imported and gets sent to the picker.

⚠ The sweep must never touch a live manifest. It would archive the controller's own file out from under a running import. The ordering that prevents it is the slot: inside the authority task, before anything can launch an import. Do not call the sweep from anywhere else.

⭐ The one-time cleanup is the same code — both droids hold several completed 08-20 manifests in imports/; first launch after this ships sweeps them. No migration.
