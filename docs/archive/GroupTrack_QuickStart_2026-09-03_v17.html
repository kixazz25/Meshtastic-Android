GroupTrack — Quick Start

Getting you onto a trail with a map that works and a ride you can keep.

This is the short version. It covers the least you need to get going. Everything else — every panel, every option — is in the User Manual, which you can open any time from Help.

What GroupTrack is

An off-road trail app for side-by-side, UTV, ATV, dirt bike and Jeep riders. It holds the trails and the maps on your phone, so it keeps working where there is no signal — which is most of where you ride.

Two kinds of rider use it. Open the one that is you.

I ride with my phone

You want trails, offline maps, and a record of where you went. Everything below is about that.

You will see radio panels you do not need. The list down the left side of the app belongs to Meshtastic, the radio software GroupTrack is built on. If you have no radio, none of it is for you. Ignore it.
Installation
1 · Allow file access and location GroupTrack asks for both on first launch. It needs file access because maps and trail data live as files on your phone, and location because it draws where you are. Tap through and grant them.
2 · Let it finish housekeeping On a first install or after an update you may see a red SYSTEM HOUSEKEEPING message. Wait for it. The Continue button turns on by itself when it is done.
3 · Load your state It asks which state you ride in and offers the one it thinks you are in. Pick yours and let it load. It takes about twenty minutes and pulls a lot of data — do it on wifi, not at the trailhead.
4 · Read the summary When it finishes you get a list of what came in. It waits for you. If a line says a source failed, the rest still loaded — you can reload the state later from the same place.
Before your first ride
1 · Know what you are looking at Solid lines are motorized. Dotted lines are not. That is the one thing worth learning before you set off. Colour separates the types within each group.
Tap Map Keys to see what each line means.
2 · Know what is hidden GroupTrack starts by showing motorized trails on public land only. Hiking and biking trails are hidden, and so is private ground.
If a trail you know exists is not there, that is why — not a fault. Change it under Map Features › Trails › SELECT.
3 · Download the maps for where you are going Trail lines are already on your phone. The satellite and topo imagery underneath them is not. Use MAPS to download the area before you leave. Without it you get trails floating on a blank background.
4 · Have a look around Pan to where you ride and tap a trail. It opens a card showing everything known about it — surface, who manages it, any posted restrictions. Some trails carry a lot; many carry almost nothing.
More in the manual: planning a route the app builds for you, changing the trail colours, and what each trail type means.
Recording your first ride

This is the part most riders want, and it is deliberately simple.

1 · Start on the Ride Map That is the map you land on. Check you can see yourself and that the imagery around you has loaded.
2 · Start recording before you set off Not once you are moving. The first part of a ride is usually the part you most want on the map afterwards.
3 · Put the phone away and ride It keeps recording with the screen off. You do not need to keep it awake or keep the app in front.
4 · Stop when you are done Your ride is saved to My tracks and drawn on the map in green. It stays on the phone — nothing needs a signal.
Battery. Recording uses GPS the whole time. On a long day, bring a charger.
More in the manual: naming and tidying your tracks, sharing a ride, exporting to GPX, and turning a ride you liked into a route to follow again.
I ride in a group with Meshtastic radios

You want everything above plus the ability to see where the rest of your group is, with no cell service. That is what the radio does.

Everyone in the group needs a radio, and every radio needs to be on the same channel. One rider without one is invisible to the others.
Installation
1 · Allow file access and location Same as any rider. Maps and trail data live as files; location draws where you are.
2 · Let it finish housekeeping A red SYSTEM HOUSEKEEPING message on a first install or after an update. Wait for it — Continue turns on by itself.
3 · Load your state About twenty minutes, on wifi. Do it at home.
4 · Pair your radio Turn the radio on and connect it over Bluetooth. The panels down the left side of the app are the radio's — that is where pairing, channels and radio settings live.
In the manual: the full radio setup, including channel settings and getting a group onto the same channel.
Before your first ride
1 · Everything the phone-only rider does Know that solid lines are motorized and dotted are not. Know that hiking trails and private land start hidden. Download the map imagery for where you are going. Those matter just as much with a radio.
2 · Get the group on one channel Radios only hear each other on a shared channel. Sort this out before the day, not in the car park.
3 · Make sure you are identifiable Your radio carries a name. Set it to something the others will recognise — a group map of unnamed nodes helps nobody.
4 · Test it with one other rider Stand apart, both with radios on, and check you can see each other move. Ten minutes at home beats finding out at the trailhead.
Recording your first ride
1 · Radio on and connected before you leave Check the app shows it connected. If it is not, you are recording a solo ride whether you meant to or not.
2 · Check you can see the others Before anyone sets off, confirm the group appears on the map. This is the moment to fix it, not three miles in.
3 · Start recording, then ride Your own ride records exactly as it does for any rider. The radio adds the others to your map; it does not change how your track is captured.
4 · Ride Positions update as people move. Range depends on terrain — a ridge between two riders will drop the link, and it comes back when it clears.
5 · Stop when you are done Your ride is saved to My tracks, same as anyone's.
More in the manual: what the radio can and cannot do, expected range, and what to check when a rider stops appearing.
