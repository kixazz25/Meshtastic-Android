⭐ One panel, one commitment.

Tile handling and reload are separate decisions but a single action. You see what you lose, what you get back, and what it costs — before anything runs.

⭐ Sections disable rather than disappear. Choosing Keep or Replace in place greys the reload section — there is nothing to reload. Same pattern as the greyed import rows: state stays visible, control goes away.

⭐ The numbers are the recommendation. No decision engine. 4,812 MB freed against 4 hours to rebuild — the choice makes itself, and it stays honest for the tester with 60 GB as well as the one with 300 MB.

⚠ Source count is the biggest lever and it has never been visible at the point of decision. Two sources instead of three is a third off the time.

⛔ No silent exit. Back-button and outside-tap do nothing. CANCEL is an explicit choice and is recorded as one — otherwise the record dangles as a permanently "interrupted" migration and pollutes the next source change on this column.

3What PROCEED runs BUILT (clear half)

1  begin()            record written — ABORT if it cannot be written
2  holdQueue()        no new jobs start
3  cancelAll()        cancels DOWNLOADING workers (keeps COMPLETE)
4  awaitIdle()        poll ≤15s — cancelWorkById is ASYNC and returns early.
                      timeout ⇒ FAIL, do not delete under a live worker
5  clearCompleted()   drains the history cancelAll deliberately kept
6  deleteSource(dir)  per OLD-source cache dir, noteDeleted() after each
7  complete()         + resumeQueue()
                      ── then the reload selection is enqueued ──  NEXT

⭐ Clear is its own job at priority 0, ahead of the batch — not a flag on the first entry. Queue order is not guaranteed at MAX_CONCURRENT = 2, so a per-entry flag means whichever job runs first wipes what the others wrote.

⭐ Clear ⇒ replace = false, forced and locked. On an empty store there is nothing to replace, and hasTile then deduplicates across the whole batch — overlapping corridors fetch shared tiles once instead of repeatedly.

⭐ Resume is free. Already-deleted dirs are skipped, so running on an interrupted record finishes rather than repeats.

⭐⭐ The answer to "where did my tiles go?"

The migration deliberately destroys the queue history — the one record that would otherwise say what had been downloaded. So the record lives in maps/source_changes/, a sibling of maps/tiles/, outside everything a clear touches.

⚠ Interrupted records prompt, never auto-resume. Finishing means deleting GB of tiles, and the launch that finds it may be at a trailhead.
