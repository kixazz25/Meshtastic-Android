The GroupTrack ride network: Nucleus backbone, cart radios, one message format

How a GroupTrack ride should work end to end with Natak hardware, and the two things we are asking Natak for: bridge support for bare mesh radios (no Android), and a bare radio to replace our Seeed T1000-E trackers.

From Fred Kix (GroupTrack) to Nathan Boyette (Natak Mesh). Based on bench and road tests 20–21 October 2026 with Nucleus node 46 (V3-OS 0.11.0), two T1000-E radios and two Android tablets.

1. The end state

Every ride runs on at least three Nucleus units — one each in the Leader, Middle and Tail gunner carts — and one bare radio in every other cart, replacing today's T1000-E. Everything a tablet or a Nucleus sends is in the ATAK format (compressed TAKPacketV2 on Meshtastic port 257, the format the Nucleus bridge already speaks). A bare radio with no tablet runs standard Meshtastic firmware and can only send its own position on port 72 (TAKPacket V1); the Nucleus translates that for its Wi-Fi riders, for backward compatibility. Updates from Wi-Fi riders reach GroupTrack on LoRa as port 257. A rider's tablet connects either to the cart's bare radio (USB or Bluetooth) or to the nearest Nucleus over Wi-Fi. At base camp, a Nucleus with a Starlink on its Ethernet WAN port is the group's one internet bridge.

LoRa: 257 ATAK, 72 from bare radios Wi-Fi / IP USB or Bluetooth to a tablet bare radio (replaces T1000-E) Nucleus

Three Nucleus units ride in the key positions; every other cart carries a bare radio. A cart with a tablet connects by USB or Bluetooth; a cart without one still reports its position. Riders near a Nucleus can use its Wi-Fi instead.

3. Messages: one format on the air

All traffic GroupTrack and the Nucleus originate is in Natak's format: compressed TAKPacketV2 on port 257 over LoRa, CoT on the Wi-Fi. GroupTrack already decodes port 257 (TAKPacket-SDK 0.9.1; all 47 SDK golden frames pass), so this is a change to what we send. Today's bridge test compressed a full CoT position to 52 bytes — less airtime than anything we send now.

Why translate at the source, not at the first receiver: every radio in range hears a packet at the same instant, so "first receiver" means several devices re-sending the same report. Translating where the message starts keeps one transmission per report. A Nucleus translating a bare radio's report puts it on its Wi-Fi only: the LoRa riders already heard the original.

The lead's gap polyline, end to end

When the lead has heard no other cart for at least 60 s, then hears one again, his tablet sends the path he really drove during that dead air: 20 points at equal time steps, from his own radio's fixes, once plus one repeat. Every receiving device processes it; nobody just forwards it blindly.

On the Wi-Fi there is no LoRa size limit, so the Nucleus should pass every vertex and the timing (t0 in the uid, the step in remarks) intact — that is what lets each device place the points in time. If a Wi-Fi rider is ever the lead, the same polyline goes the other way through your existing CoT → 257 path.

4. What we are asking Natak for

ACode: support bare mesh radios (no Android) in the Nucleus

  1. Translate LoRa port 72 (TAKPacket V1) to CoT on the Wi-Fi — the position reports every bare radio sends by itself (today's T1000-E and the new bare radio alike), so a Wi-Fi rider sees bare-radio carts. This is the backward-compatible path: a bare radio cannot send 257 without an Android. Today cot_bridge handles only ATAK_FORWARDER (257), text, voice and heartbeat; our standalone T1000-E was heard by node 46 (SNR 11) but nothing reached the Wi-Fi. Mapping in the appendix.
  2. Loop guard: a CoT the Nucleus put on the Wi-Fi from LoRa must not go back out on LoRa as 257. (Your TX source filter takes multicast from 10.20.46.0/24, and the box itself is in that range.)
  3. One rider, one id: keep our uid MESHTASTIC-!<8-hex node id> for a bare radio, so a cart heard directly and through a bridge is one cart everywhere.
  4. The lead's gap polyline: decode a 257 CoT u-d-f drawn shape and hand the full polyline to the Wi-Fi as one CoT drawing (every vertex as a link point, uid and remarks intact), not back to LoRa — see "The lead's gap polyline, end to end" above. And confirm the reverse (CoT u-d-f → 257) carries every vertex up to the 32-point limit.
  5. Our encoder: the TAKPacket-SDK version and dictionary to compress with, and your size limit per LoRa packet.
  6. Box items found on the bench: bridge health stays "error" after a clean radio reconnect; the box clock read 7 October with no internet (CoT time stamps would look stale); which V3-OS version to test against (46 is on 0.11.0 — can 46/47 self-update to 0.16.0 over the Ethernet WAN?); API security before a tablet writes to a box.
  7. TX rate limit: we will set your tx_min_interval_secs to 5 s for GroupTrack rides (our carts report every 6 s). Any airtime concern at MEDIUM_FAST?

BHardware: a bare radio to replace the T1000-E

A rip-and-replace for our T1000-E trackers, one per cart, with the same mounting and antenna requirements as the Nucleus:

  • Meshtastic-compatible LoRa radio on the GroupTrack ride network (preset, channel, key set by GroupTrack at check-in).
  • GPS chip — required. With no tablet attached it reports its own cart's position.
  • USB communications — USB for power and data: GroupTrack connects a tablet by cable and configures the radio over it. (Bluetooth as well, if it adds little cost.)
  • Power from the cart's USB (5 V), surviving the ignition being switched off — a radio board rather than a Pi, so no boot wait and no corrupted card.
  • Mounting and antenna, as the Nucleus: cart mount (roof or roll-bar), weather-resistant, external antenna connector, a cart-mount whip, and a spare antenna in the kit.
  • Message format: standard Meshtastic firmware, role TAK_TRACKER: with no tablet it sends its position on port 72, which the Nucleus translates (request A1). With a GroupTrack tablet attached, the tablet sends for the cart in ATAK format (257) and the radio's own reports are switched off. No custom firmware needed.
  • Transmit power within the licence-free limit; GroupTrack never raises it.
  • Price that lets a club equip ten carts — no Wi-Fi mesh or extras on this unit.

CThe Nucleus in the three key roles

  • Minimum three per ride: Leader, Middle, Tail gunner — same cart mount, external antenna and spare antenna as the bare radio; USB power from the cart (our 10-11 plan: cart USB charges a 20,000 mAh jump-starter pack that powers the unit).
  • Base camp: Ethernet WAN to a Starlink as the group's one internet bridge.
  • Questions: Nucleus-to-Nucleus Wi-Fi range between moving carts; rough prices for both units; earliest test units; can units ship pre-set to the GroupTrack default network?

5. What GroupTrack does on its side

  • Send in your format: positions, role reports and the gap polyline as compressed TAKPacketV2 on 257 (we already decode it).
  • A tablet-attached radio stops reporting itself: at check-in its role becomes TAK (as on the Nucleus), so each cart sends one report.
  • A Nucleus path in our comm layer beside Bluetooth: a tablet on Nucleus Wi-Fi sends and receives CoT on 239.2.3.1:6969, and at check-in applies the ride's radio settings to the box through /api/v1/meshtastic/config/apply, verified by psk_fingerprint (first 8 hex of SHA-256 of the key — checked today).
  • Bridged riders on the map: 257 riders are decoded today but only logged; they join the map.
  • Side-by-side range test of the new bare radio against a T1000-E on our measured loop.

6. What we proved on 20–21 October

  • Done Node 46's LoRa radio put on the GroupTrack network through your API only (config/apply: preset, channel name, key); factory config saved first; your node-only values untouched; one 10 s poll; read-back and key fingerprint matched.
  • Done LoRa both ways: 46 heard our standalone T1000-E (SNR 11); a text from 46 reached our rider.
  • Done Wi-Fi to LoRa: a CoT position from a tablet on 46's Wi-Fi left as a 52-byte ATAK packet (TX → LoRa | GT-wifi-test-1 | a-f-G-U-C | 52B).
  • Gap: LoRa to Wi-Fi for the T1000-E (port 72) — request A1.
  • Range today (T1000-E to T1000-E, dense housing, New Hampshire granite): reliable ~470–550 yd; Long Fast only +10–30%. Utah rides keep carts within about a mile — the reason for request B.
  • Lead path through a dead spot: GroupTrack's gap polyline (20 points, ~205 bytes uncompressed) crossed the mesh both ways and corrected the lead's track on the road (6-minute gap, received 2 s after reconnect).

Appendix — formats on the air today

Our ride network

Channel GroupTrack, region US, MEDIUM_FAST, frequency slot 0 (derived from the channel name), hop limit 2 on our radios, rebroadcast ALL. Standard Meshtastic channel encryption: AES-256 with a 32-byte key on the primary channel (sent to you separately, not in this document).

Port 72 — ATAK_PLUGIN — TAKPacket (V1), meshtastic/atak.proto

Sent by the T1000-E firmware (2.6.11, TAK_TRACKER) every 6 s moving / 30 s stopped, is_compressed = true; and by GroupTrack role reports, is_compressed = false.

is_compressed (1) · contact (2) {callsign (1), device_callsign (2)} · group (3) {role (1), team (2)} · status (4) {battery (1)} · pli (5) {latitude_i (1) sfixed32 ×1e-7°, longitude_i (2) sfixed32 ×1e-7°, altitude (3) int32 m, speed (4), course (5) as the firmware fills them}

Roles: TeamLead = Leader · RTO = Middle · ForwardObserver = Tail gunner · HQ = Rider · TeamMember = no ride role. Team: Cyan.
CoT we would like on the Wi-Fi: uid MESHTASTIC-!<node>, type a-f-G-U-C, how m-g, point from pli, hae = altitude, time = start = receive time, stale = time + 2× reporting interval, <contact callsign> (decompressed), <__group name role>, <status battery>, <track speed course>.

Port 78 — ATAK_PLUGIN_V2 — the gap polyline (moving to 257)

flag 0xFF + TAKPacketV2: cot_type_id (1) = CotType_u_d_f · callsign (3) · uid (14) = "GT-gap-<node>-<t0 epoch s>" · latitude_i (6) / longitude_i (7) = anchor (first vertex) · stale_seconds (16) = 600 · remarks (24) = "GT gap step=<ms>" · shape (34) DrawnShape {kind (1) = Kind_Freeform, vertex_lat_deltas (18) / vertex_lon_deltas (19) packed sint32 1e-7° from the anchor, ≤32 (we send 20)}
