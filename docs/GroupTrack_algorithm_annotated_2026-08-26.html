The route search — annotated

2026-08-26 · ALGORITHM-2026-08-26 · every step, the rule it applies, and why · measured against spatial_after.db

⛔ THIS REPLACES THE RANDOMISED GREEDY. The shipping engine runs 2,500 restarts of a gain-per-mile greedy with a stochastic beam pick and a route-level PENALTY. None of that survives. The new search is enumerate, filter, score — deterministic, explainable, and it can say WHY a feature was rejected.

1 Read the corridor

RULE. A box of milesHigh / 3 around the staging point.

⭐ Why a third. Measured 2026-08-23: an 80-mile round trip reaches about 25–30 mi out, so the ceiling divided by three is the shape. The previous formula — half the ceiling, scaled, plus a margin — gave a 70×70 box, 53,711 edges and an OutOfMemoryError building 112 Dijkstra tables on a 256 MB heap.

⚠ Do not widen this without measuring edges. 53,711 is the number that died. At SNAP_FT 50 the same corridor produces 51,569 — within 4% of it.

Measured: 8,065 trails at 37.7235,-112.613.

2 Build the graph

RULE. Two passes. A: any grid cell holding vertices from 2+ trails is a junction. B: split every trail at its endpoints and at every junction.

⭐ Intersection splitting already exists and works. An earlier prototype compared only ENDPOINTS, reported 114 components, and sent us chasing a connectivity problem the app does not have. Pass A uses every vertex.

⚠ cellKey is a GRID, not a radius. Two points six feet apart land in different cells when they straddle a boundary. Measured on the map: a 6 ft gap at a trailhead was not joined. Widening the cell halves how often that happens but never removes it — the fix is checking the eight neighbouring cells, and it is not built.

⚠ SNAP_FT is a HARD CEILING on how far apart two trails can be digitised and still join. At 25 ft, measured 30 ft and 60 ft gaps can never join. Raising it to 50 was tried and reverted 08-26: +53.5% edges for no measured benefit — spokes stayed at 1 at every tolerance from 25 to 100.

Measured: 24,877 nodes, 40,115 edges, 635 components, largest 12,231.

3 Find the candidate starts

RULE. Every trail passing within 600 yards of the staging point is a candidate start. Split that trail at the point it passes closest, and use the new node as an origin.

⭐ This is the defect the whole of 08-25 traced back to. nearestNode returns the closest EXISTING node — nodes only exist at junctions, so a tap mid-trail is dragged to whichever end is nearer. Measured: 613 ft from the staging point, with ONE spoke. Every one of the 2,500 restarts then began down that single spoke, and the other directions were not under-explored — they were structurally excluded.

⭐ Fred's framing. A staging area is the hub of a spoked wheel. Picking one arbitrary node picks one spoke.

⚠ Why 600 yards. Measured at 37.8189,-112.416: 200 yds gave 1 trail, 1 component; 300 gave 3 trails and 2 components; 600 gave 12 trails and 5 components. Below 300 the only network reachable was a six-node island. A fixed distance, not "collect until you have N components" — that would give a different radius depending on how local topology happens to fall.

⭐ Not a trailhead — a staging area. The word matters: "trailhead" implies a trail begins there, and it does not. A rider picks the parking lot, which is obvious from satellite imagery and needs no trail knowledge.

Measured: 13 candidate starts, all with 2 spokes, against nearestNode's single node at 179 ft.

3b Collapse origins to one per component

RULE. One origin per connected component, the closest. Every other origin on that component is recorded as an alternative start for the same ride.

⭐ Why. Thirteen origins at 37.7235,-112.613 all had the SAME 13 usable POIs — thirteen car parks onto one network. Solving every combination thirteen times to get thirteen near-identical answers cost 15,093 tour solves at one size alone, and the dedupe collapsed them afterwards anyway.

⚠ The alternatives must be KEPT, not discarded. Throwing them away made the origin column read "1 of 5" and the multi-origin search look broken when it was working. A rider deciding where to park needs every start a ride can be reached from.

4 Load and attach the features

RULE. reference_points inside the box, minus JUNK (county, city, town, tree, village), attached to the nearest graph node within POI_BUF_MI = 0.5.

⛔ NOT to mainComponent. The shipping code attaches POIs only to the largest component. Measured 08-26: that component held 1 of Fred's ten pins while another held 7. "Largest" is not "the one the rider is near", and binding POIs to it makes most of the map invisible.

⚠ MAX_POI matters more than it looks. Capping at 14 gave rides of 3 features; at 24 the same location gave rides of 7. The cap was discarding the variety.

Measured: 274 rows, 99 attached to 93 nodes.

5 Distance matrix

RULE. One Dijkstra per terminal — every origin and every feature node — giving dm[(a,b)] for all pairs.

⚠ This is the memory risk. One table is one entry per node per terminal. The 08-23 OOM was 112 tables on a 256 MB heap; the pin cap of ten and the collapse at step 3b are what bound it now.

5b Eliminate features by out-and-back

RULE. For each origin, drop any feature where dm[(origin,poi)] + dm[(poi,origin)] > ceiling. An origin left with nothing is dropped entirely.

⭐ Why this is the cheapest possible filter. Removing ONE feature removes every combination containing it. Dropping 1 of 14 loses 8,192 subsets; dropping 4 leaves 1,024 instead of 16,384 — and none of them are ever generated. A per-combination test still has to enumerate all 16,384 to reject them.

⚠ Conservative by design. This only removes features impossible ON THEIR OWN. A feature at 45 mi out-and-back survives here and will still bust when paired with anything else — that is what step 5c is for.

Measured: 24 features to 13; 24 origins to 13.

5c ⭐ PAIRWISE TABLE — not yet built

⚠ THIS IS THE MISSING STEP, and it is where the time goes.

RULE. For every pair of surviving features, the minimum ride containing both: dm[(o,A)] + dm[(A,B)] + dm[(B,o)]. That is 78 numbers for 13 features, from the matrix already computed.

Any combination containing a pair that busts the ceiling is dead — at EVERY size. If features 3 and 9 cannot share a ride, every combination containing both disappears at once, without being generated.

⭐ And it gives the maximum feasible size directly. Measured 08-26: sizes 13 down to 6 produced nothing — 3,302 combinations generated, tested and rejected, consuming most of the 67.8 seconds. All six rides appeared at size 5. The pair table would have said "5" in 78 lookups.

⚠ The existing bound is weaker. It uses 2 × largest pairwise distance, which underestimates because it ignores reaching the origin and returning.

6 Enumerate, filter, score

RULE. Descend by feature count from the largest set down. For each combination, one path per origin. Keep only rides between floor and ceiling. Score the survivors.

⭐ Why descend from all. If the full set fits, it is the answer by construction — most features collected. Dropping a level is only necessary when the full set is too long.

⭐ One path per combination per origin. Not 2–3 routings through the same features: the variation comes from the origins, which is the point of multi-start.

⚠ Exact tour up to 8 features, nearest-neighbour above. Permutations of 9+ are not affordable and the answer only has to be good enough to FILTER on.

Ordering and stopping

RULE. Within a feature count, longest ride first. Stop once six distinct rides are found.

⭐ A rider who asked for up to 80 is offered the fullest ride at each level before shorter ones. And the descent is largest-first, so anything below collects fewer features and scores lower by construction — once six are banked, everything below is wasted work.

⚠ Six DISTINCT rides, counted on the feature set — not six raw candidates, which from one component could be the same ride six times.

7 Dedupe, in two passes

PASS 1. Same origin AND same feature set, within 5 miles → keep the highest mileage.

Near-duplicates of one ride found by slightly different tours.

PASS 2. Same feature set, ANY origin, within 5 miles → keep the highest mileage, and record every origin it was found from.

⭐ Measured: Pole Hollow, Little Valley Road and an unnamed trail returned the same five knolls at 79.6 / 79.6 / 79.5. One ride, three car parks.

⛔ WHAT PASS 2 MUST NOT DO: collapse rides that reach DIFFERENT features. A trail heading the opposite way is a genuinely different ride at the same mileage, and that is the entire point of multi-origin.

⚠ Highest mileage, not shortest — the rider asked for up to 80.

⛔ NO RANKING. Fred, 08-26: "we don't rank, we present 6 recommended rides." The score's only job is getting six good candidates into the list — it never faces the rider. That is why "2 of 6 unique scores" was never a real problem: the rider compares by WHAT IS ON each ride, and the feature sets are 6 of 6 distinct.

⭐ The rider has the narrative and the display with all routes on it, and decides what to make permanent. No "best", no recommendation of a recommendation.

⚠ WHAT PHASE 1 MUST CARRY WITH IT — these are not optional, each was measured:

  • Candidate starts within 600 yds, edge-split at the closest approach — not nearestNode
  • Collapse to one origin per component, keeping the alternatives for display
  • POIs attach to any component an origin is on — NOT mainComponent
  • Out-and-back filter (5b) and the pairwise table (5c) — together these took 67.8s to 3.8s
  • Spatial grid for POI attachment — 5.8s to 0.1s. nearestNode has the same defect and the same fix.
  • Descend from the clique bound, longest ride first within a size, stop at six distinct
  • Both dedupe passes, pass 2 keeping the origins it collapses

⭐ SETTLED 08-26 — the ordering, and what is still owed

THE ALGORITHM, STATED PLAINLY:

Filter to feasible → solve in points-descending order → keep what fits the band → stop at six → present ranked by points, then mileage descending.

⭐ Points are the only SEARCH ordering. scoreSet depends only on which features are in a set, not on the route through them — so candidates can be sorted before a single tour is solved. Six solves of the thing that costs anything, not thousands.

⚠ DISTANCE IS A FILTER, NEVER A SORT KEY during the search. Fred, 08-26: "you cannot take distance until completion of a score. each route may expand beyond the minimum." The pair table gives a MINIMUM; a combination bounded at 72 may solve to 78 or to 65 and fail the floor. Actual mileage exists only once the tour is done.

⭐ Mileage ranks only AMONG EQUALS, at presentation. Two rides collecting the same features: the longer wins, because a rider who asked for up to 80 wants the fullest ride.

⚠ STOP-AT-SIX IS CHECKED BETWEEN SIZES, NOT INSIDE ONE. So the current size completes before stopping. At the top size in a POI-dense area that is the blow-up — C(30,15) is 155 million. Solving in points order and checking after each solve removes it entirely.

⚠ And it may be worth solving ten, presenting six. Stopping at exactly six means a seventh solve might have found a longer ride at the same points — so "longest at each point level" is approximately true rather than guaranteed. Ten solves instead of six costs nothing.

⭐⭐ OWED: ROUTE-DIVERSITY DEDUPE. Fred, 08-26: "if 60 miles of an 80 mile trail are the same trails to the same POIs, are they really different routes?"

Today's dedupe compares the POI set and the mileage. It gets BOTH errors:

  • Two rides on the same trails, differing only at the end, are kept as different
  • Two rides reaching the same features by genuinely different trails — which IS diversity — would be collapsed

⭐ Edge overlap fixes both. Every ride is already a list of edge ids (eids in picks), so |A ∩ B| / |A ∪ B| is computable from data already in hand. Two rides sharing most of their edges are one ride.

⚠ The threshold is empirical — 50%? 70%? — and the comparison panel is exactly what would show it: shared versus unique trails per ride. Add the dedupe section once results are being evaluated, not before.

⭐ SCORING BY SOMETHING OTHER THAN FEATURES — Fred, 08-26. POI scoring assumes a ride's value is what you stop and look at. For OHV riding it may mostly be what the trail is like to ride. Candidates:

  • Difficulty — GPX carries elevation, so climb and grade are derivable from what is already imported. Surface is ~30% populated in OSM.
  • Popularity — in no current source. The track survey (enjoyment, ride_again) is the only rider-supplied signal that exists.
  • Proximity to services — reference_points.fclass may already carry this. Worth listing the actual fclass values before designing anything.

⚠ And a different SHAPE of ride: "20–30 miles to lunch and then home" is not a loop optimising features — it is a destination ride where the POI that matters is a café and the metric is somewhere to eat at roughly half distance.

⚠ AREAS WITH NO FEATURES AT ALL. With poiNodes empty every combination is empty and the engine returns nothing. Fred's answer: require a pin. "We don't have any named features here — drop a pin where you'd like the ride to go." The rider supplies the target the data lacks, and the existing pipeline runs unchanged.

⚠ Probably a threshold, not a zero test — one feature in range is barely better than none. And one pin gives one destination, so the six rides would differ only by route: ask for two or three.

Open
