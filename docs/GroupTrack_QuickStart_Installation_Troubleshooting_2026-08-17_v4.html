GroupTrack — Quick Start, Installation & Troubleshooting
Beta Tester Reference · Updated August 17, 2026 (V2.6f)

⚠️ UPDATE FIRST — DO NOT UNINSTALL ON AN OLD BUILD.

Before following any recovery step in this document, make sure you are on Rel 2.6f or later (Settings shows the release). On builds before 2.6f, uninstalling and reinstalling erased saved trails, tracks and waypoints — a one-time database upgrade step could not tell a reinstall from a brand-new install and cleared the databases. Downloaded map tiles were not affected. 2.6f fixes this, and additionally refuses to clear a database that still holds records. If you are on an older build, take the update from Play first — an update is safe and never triggered this.

Which mode are you running?

GroupTrack works two ways. You do not need a radio to use the app. Pick the mode that matches your situation — everything else in this document is the same for both.

1 · Solo mode — Android GPS DEFAULT

Your phone or tablet's own GPS provides your position. No Meshtastic radio, no Bluetooth pairing, no radio firmware to worry about. You get offline maps, trail data, route planning and track recording — everything except seeing other riders.

This is the default and it is what most testers should start with. If you have never used GroupTrack before, use solo mode until you are comfortable with the app.

2 · Group mode — mesh radios OPTIONAL

Adds live tracking of everyone else in your convoy. Every rider you want to see needs their own Meshtastic radio paired to their own device — the radios form the mesh that carries positions where there is no cell signal.

Group mode is an addition to solo mode, not a replacement. Set the app up and get comfortable first; add the radio afterwards. See Adding a radio below.

FIRST RUN — do these steps in order

Steps 1–4 build on each other and should be done in order. Step 5 is optional. The in-app manual is the reference throughout — this document tells you the order, the manual tells you the detail.

Step 1 · Open the manual and get familiar with it

Open the manual from the "?" in the app and spend a few minutes on its structure — what sections exist and roughly where things live. You are not reading it cover to cover. You are learning where to look, because the next three steps each send you back to it.

Everything else in this quick start assumes you can find a topic in the manual when pointed at one.

Step 2 · Import trails

Open the hamburger panel and go to the artifacts display — this is where trails, tracks, routes and waypoints are managed.

Read the manual's "Import trails" section before you start. Then, in the app:

  1. Select a source. Use the first entry — UGRC Utah Trails — as your starting point. (This is the Utah Geospatial Resource Center's curated trail dataset.)
  2. Validate the source.
  3. Process the import.

This gives you a curated, agency-maintained trail set with use classifications — the coloured trails you will see on the map. It is the smaller of the two trail sources but the better-described one.

Step 3 · Import Open Source Maps (OSM) trail data

Look up the Open Source Maps import function in the manual first, then follow the prompts and screens in the app. It is a four-stage process on a single panel and the app tells you what it needs at each stage.

This adds substantially more ground than the curated source alone — for Utah roughly 104,000 trails and unpaved roads, against about 48,000 from the state source.

Two things the prompts will not tell you:

  • Take the file ending -latest-free.gpkg.zip (about 330 MB for Utah). The other formats on that page will download and then be rejected.
  • When the download finishes, tap the green bar yourself. Nothing happens automatically — GroupTrack does not watch your downloads folder.

Step 4 · Check your map source

Open the map source settings and confirm what you are using. The default values are chosen for performance and usability — leave them alone unless you have a specific reason.

If you do change a source, the app will tell you what happens to any tiles you have already downloaded. Read that screen rather than clicking through it.

Step 5 · Bring in your own tracks and waypoints OPTIONAL

If you already have riding history in onX, Gaia or another app, you can bring it across. Nothing is lost by skipping this — but your own past rides are the most useful trail data you own, because you know what they were like.

First, get the GPX files onto this device. Two routes, depending on where the other app lives:

  • The other app is on this Android device — export to GPX from within it and download the files here.
  • The other app is elsewhere (a different phone, a desktop) — export to GPX and email the files to yourself as attachments, then open the mail on this device and download them.

Either way the GPX files end up in this device's downloads. GroupTrack does not reach into onX or Gaia — it reads files you have already put on the device.

Then import them:

  1. Select Import artifacts. Your downloaded tracks will be listed.
  2. Tick the tracks you want. You do not have to take everything you exported.
  3. Tap Import selected at the bottom of the panel to process them.

Imported tracks behave like tracks you recorded yourself — they appear on the map, carry distance and time, and can be used for planning.

After steps 1–4 you have the app, the manual, trail data from two sources, and a working map — a complete solo-mode setup. Download map tiles for the area you ride and you are ready. Step 5 is optional and can be done at any time, including months later.

Adding a radio OPTIONAL — GROUP MODE ONLY

Skip this entire section if you are running solo. Do it last, after maps and trails are in place and you are comfortable with the app. The manual covers pairing under Radio / Connection.

⚠️ T1000-E firmware: DO NOT update beyond 2.6.11.

T1000-E firmware must stay at 2.6.11 or earlier. Any newer version destabilises Bluetooth connectivity and radio configuration — a known issue with newer Meshtastic firmware on this hardware.

Check it in the Meshtastic app under Radio Config → Device Info. If it is 2.6.11 or earlier, do not update. If it was updated by accident, flash back to 2.6.11 with the Meshtastic Flasher tool. This applies to every T1000-E used with GroupTrack.

⚠️ Keep the radio within Bluetooth range during rides.

Keep your radio in your cart or vehicle, within about 50 feet of your phone, for the whole ride. Carrying one away from the other breaks the Bluetooth link and the app will pause or freeze until they are back in range.

Upgrading testers — one-time tile migration

Only if you are coming from a build earlier than V2.6. Do this before downloading any tiles.

V2.6 changed how offline tiles are stored — from thousands of loose files to one compact database per map type, roughly half the storage (satellite about 55% smaller). Old loose tiles must be cleared once and re-downloaded in the new format.

  1. Install X-plore File Manager (free, Play Store). It shows a live progress count while deleting, so you can see it working.
  2. In X-plore go to Internal storage → Documents → GroupTrack → maps and delete the maps folder. A large old cache takes a while — keep the device plugged in.
  3. Back in GroupTrack, download the areas you need again. They are saved in the new format at roughly half the previous size.

Your tracks, trails and routes are not affected — this clears downloaded map imagery only. It is a one-time step; future updates will not need it.

TROUBLESHOOTING

Map and trail display

Trails do not appear. Check you completed Step 2 and Step 3 — with no trail import there is nothing to draw. Then check your zoom level; trail detail appears as you zoom in.

Map is blank or grey offline. You have no downloaded tiles for that area at that zoom. Download the area while you have a connection.

App behaviour

Missing data after an update. Your saved content is stored outside the app. It always survives updates; on 2.6f and later it also survives a full uninstall and reinstall. If trails or tracks are missing after an update, re-check the artifacts panel before assuming loss — and see the warning at the top of this document if you are on an older build.

App asks for storage access on every launch. Grant "all files access" when asked. GroupTrack keeps 50–60 GB of offline maps in shared storage so they survive updates and reinstalls, which is why it needs that permission. Without it the app cannot open your maps, trails or tracks and will not start.

Location prompt keeps appearing. If you skipped "allow all the time", GroupTrack reminds you each launch. That is deliberate: without it, track recording stops when your screen sleeps, and you will not find out until you get home to a short track. Grant it in Android settings whenever you like.

Radio connection GROUP MODE ONLY

App freezes or pauses during a ride. The radio moved out of Bluetooth range while still connected. Force close GroupTrack, bring the radio back within range, reopen. Prevention: radio stays in the cart with the phone.

Radio connects then disconnects every 8–10 seconds. Device hardware incompatibility — supervision timeout reason 0x0008, seen on budget tablets without a proper BLE radio. Use a device that meets the requirements above. This is hardware, not an app bug.

Radio will not connect after a firmware update. Firmware went beyond 2.6.11. Flash back to 2.6.11 with the Meshtastic Flasher tool.

Emergency recovery

Check your version first. Everything below assumes 2.6f or later. On earlier builds an uninstall erased saved trails, tracks and waypoints — take the update from Play before doing any of this.

If the app is badly broken and an update has not fixed it:

  1. Uninstall GroupTrack.
  2. Reinstall from Play.
  3. Grant all-files access when asked.
  4. Launch. Your trails, tracks, routes and waypoints in Documents/GroupTrack/ will still be there.

Downloaded map tiles do not survive an uninstall on any build — you will re-download those.
