Track design — the lead track and my track

Design notes, 2026-09-28 v1 · to be settled with the January field tests in Utah · internal (resume context)

Purpose

When the ride starts, every tablet can show up to three lines: the route (the plan), the lead track (where the Leader has been) and my track (where I have been). This document records what each one is, what it costs, where it goes wrong, and the choices still to make — so that when we get to the track work, we start from what we already know rather than re-covering it.

The three lines

LineSourceRadio trafficAccuracyWhere it is seen
The routeThe ride's route, put on the ride map at check-in (with its trailhead, fitted)NoneExact — the planned pathEvery tablet, from check-in
The lead trackThe Leader's radio position reports (TAK reports, every 4 s moving / 15 s stopped), joined with straight linesNone extra — the Leader's radio sends these reports anyway, like every radioGood when reports are frequent; cuts corners when they are sparse (see below)Every tablet, live
My trackThe tablet's own GPS, about once a secondNone — recorded and drawn entirely on the tabletFollows the trail precisely — dense enough to hold every bend and switchback with no snappingBehind my cart, on my map, live; saved at END as the ride's track

Facts established (09-28)

FACTThe lead track adds no radio traffic. It is drawn from the Leader's normal position reports, which every radio sends regardless (Fred, 09-28). An earlier statement that it "depends on radio traffic" was wrong: the only dependency is reception.
FACTReception gaps. Out of range of the Leader, the lead track has gaps. The existing proxy snap (the lead track borrows the nearest cart's position after a stale tick) covers short gaps.
FACTLarge groups space the reports out (Fred, 09-28). With many radios sharing the channel, reports are delayed or dropped; at 15 s or more between points, straight lines cut across switchbacks and bends, and the lead track looks off the trail even when the Leader never left it — misleading exactly where it matters.
FACTMy track is the accurate record. About one GPS point a second from the tablet itself; no radio involved. The code keeps it separately from the lead track (the GPS trail segments). It is what gets saved at END — named after the ride, rated, and optionally shared.
FACTThe Leader is now known from the ride roles. Proven 09-28: the special roles (Leader → TAK Team Lead, Middle → RTO, Tail gunner → Forward Observer) travel in a standard TAK position report that the tablet fills in; every tablet lifts the role and the tick makes that cart the lead / tail. So the lead track follows the assigned Leader, not a guess.

Options

OptionStrengthsWeaknessesFits
A. Route + my track (lead track off)Exact plan; exact personal record; clean map; unaffected by group sizeDoes not show a deviation from the plan (detour, closed trail)Planned rides, especially large groups
B. Route + my track + lead trackShows the plan and where the Leader actually wentLead track cuts corners when reports are sparse; busier mapSmall groups; rides likely to leave the plan
C. Lead track + my track (no route)Works with no planned routeOnly as good as the Leader's reception and report rateImprovised rides, No scheduled ride groups
D. Lead track snapped to the trail networkRemoves the corner cuttingA substantial feature (map-matching against the trail data); only worth it if riders really want the live lead trackLater, if field tests show demand

Proposed defaults (to be confirmed in the field)

PROPOSED
  • Large groups on a planned route: A — route + my track, lead track off.
  • Small groups, or an improvised ride: B or C — lead track on.
  • The switch already exists: Show Lead Track — the Leader (or each rider) turns it on when the ride goes off plan.
  • My track is always on — it is the accurate record and costs nothing.

Fred, 09-28: "we will let it play out." Nothing here is decided until the field tests.

Open questions — January field tests (Utah)

OPEN
  • On a planned ride, do riders want the Leader's live track, or just the route?
  • In a large group, what report interval do we actually see, and how badly does the lead track cut corners?
  • Does my track draw behind my cart live, following the trail, independent of the lead track? (confirm on the trail)
  • Is the proxy snap enough for the gaps we see, or do riders read the gaps as errors?
  • Is a snapped lead track (D) worth building?

Rejected — and why

CORRECTED"The lead track depends on radio traffic." Wrong: it uses the reports the Leader's radio sends anyway. Its only dependency is reception. (Fred, 09-28)

Related (09-28)

  • Ride roles: the special roles broadcast in a tablet-filled TAK position report — every 30 s from check-in until 3 minutes after REC, then silent; fixed until reassigned (a reassignment broadcasts, HQ = Rider) or END. Blank role = Rider.
  • Trailhead order: everybody checks in first thing (the check-in puts each radio on the ride's network); special roles among the first; the ride starts when everyone has checked in.
