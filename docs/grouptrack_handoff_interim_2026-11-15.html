⭐ THE DISTRIBUTION RULE, IN ONE SENTENCE

Only RIDER trails marked shared travel. Geometry collapses by the alias rule. Surveys never collapse.

Why the rider-trail CATEGORY is the whole mechanism (Fred 10:49): OSM/Geofabrik-derived trails can never be distributed — AWS_SpatialMirror_v2 carries the hard rule: "⛓ NO OSM-DERIVED DATA ON GROUPTRACK INFRASTRUCTURE. ODbL share-alike. Rows imported from Geofabrik/OSM sources must NEVER be uploaded to these tables. Enforced in the 3.0 upload path." Rider trails — trails grown from riders' own recorded tracks — carry no such restriction. So the category is not a convenience, it is the licensing firewall, and the upload filter is a single predicate:

WHERE source_id = <RIDER> AND shared = 1

✅ Safe by default, because shared defaults to 0 and every agency-sourced trail fails the first clause. A bug in the filter omits data rather than leaking it — the correct direction for a licensing constraint.

And the asymmetry is the product (Fred 09-22, verbatim in the mirror): "Collapse the GEOMETRY (the alias rule: 20 riders / same path / same day → one community alias) — NEVER the surveys: every rider's survey counts once." Twenty riders on one trail yield one trail and twenty ratings. That is the thing OSM can never provide, and it is what a subscriber is paying for.

THE REFRESH RULES:
· Trails update ALWAYS.
· Maps update ALWAYS.
· Artifacts: the device sends its artifact list UP, and only NEW artifacts come down.

Why this is better than timestamps, not merely simpler: the two layers that "always update" come from authoritative external publishers — there is nothing to reconcile, so re-pulling is the correct behaviour and tracking currency would only let us be wrong about it. The one layer with real volume is the community artifacts, and there the device's own hash set is already the delta. No timestamps, no versions, no new columns: has_tiles and has_spatial stay as plain "do I have this at all" flags.

One design consequence worth noting: the delta is device-driven — the device says what it holds and the server answers with the remainder. That keeps the server stateless about the device: no per-device ledger, no subscription cursors, nothing to get out of step. A device that is wiped and restored simply sends a shorter list and gets more back. Detail deferred to when the item is addressed, as Fred directed.

⭐ "No sense in populating them unless you're going to ride there" is the whole justification for area scoping — and it also settles what this operation IS. It is not a sync. It is an ACQUISITION, driven by intent. A sync tries to converge two stores and needs conflict rules in both directions; an acquisition pulls what the rider asked for and needs none. That is why the download direction stays simple even though 2.9 now has a return path: the upload is a contribution, the download is an acquisition, and neither is a merge.

⚠ GAP THIS EXPOSES: two flags for five things. ride_areas carries has_tiles and has_spatial. ⭐ WHY THE STUB IS THE KEY IDEA, AND IT IS NOT CAUTION.

  • It turns a removal into a FALSIFIABLE CLAIM. Verification says the code is dead; the stub proves it in production. If rasterProcessing() ever announces itself, the verification was wrong — and that arrives as a log line instead of a crash. A plain deletion offers no such feedback: it is right or it is an incident.

  • It marks the seam. A reader who finds the stub learns what was there and when it went — the opposite of a silent deletion, and it is why the 2.7c nav flattening left us 16 orphan routes with no record of why.
